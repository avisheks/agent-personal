## 2. Company Overview

### Business Description

Reddit, Inc. operates one of the largest online community platforms in the world. Founded in 2005 and headquartered in San Francisco, Reddit hosts millions of communities ("subreddits") where users share content, engage in discussions, and vote on posts and comments across virtually every topic imaginable. The platform's unique structure — user-created and user-moderated communities organized by interest — creates powerful network effects and deep engagement moats.

### Business Model

Reddit generates revenue through three primary channels:

1. **Advertising (Primary):** Reddit sells advertising placements across its platform, including promoted posts, display ads, and video ads. Advertisers value Reddit's highly engaged, intent-rich audiences organized by interest communities. This remains the dominant revenue source.

2. **Data Licensing (Emerging):** Reddit licenses its vast corpus of human-generated conversational data to third parties, most notably AI companies training large language models. This revenue stream emerged as a significant contributor following major licensing deals with companies like Google and OpenAI. Reddit's data is uniquely valuable because it represents authentic, diverse, human-authored discussion across millions of topics.

3. **Premium Subscriptions:** Reddit Premium and community-level awards/memberships contribute a smaller but recurring revenue stream.

### Competitive Position

Reddit occupies a distinctive niche in the social media landscape:

- **Community moat:** Unlike algorithmically-driven feeds (Instagram, TikTok), Reddit's value derives from accumulated community knowledge, culture, and moderation structures built over nearly two decades. This content cannot be easily replicated.
- **Search integration:** Reddit content increasingly dominates Google search results for product reviews, recommendations, and authentic opinions — driving organic traffic growth.
- **AI data advantage:** Reddit's corpus of human discussion is among the most valuable training datasets for large language models, creating a durable monetization opportunity.

### Profitability Transformation

Reddit's profitability trajectory represents one of the most dramatic turnarounds in recent social media history:

### Key Profitability Observations

**The $1B+ swing:** Reddit went from a -$484.3M net loss in FY2024 to +$529.7M net income in FY2025 — a positive swing of over $1 billion in a single year. This is an extraordinary achievement that reflects both revenue scale and operating leverage.

**Net margin of ~24%:** For FY2025, Reddit achieved a net margin of approximately 24% ($529.7M / $2,203M). This is a strong margin for a social media company and indicates the business model is now generating substantial profit from each incremental revenue dollar.

**EPS trajectory:** Quarterly EPS of $1.01 (Q1 2026) and $1.25 (Q2 2026) implies an annualized EPS run rate of approximately $4.50-$5.00, which is well above the $2.62 full-year 2025 figure. This rapid EPS expansion is what drives the attractive forward P/E of 16.3x.

**FY2024 loss context:** The -$484.3M loss in FY2024 likely includes significant stock-based compensation expenses related to the March 2024 IPO. This is a common pattern — IPO-year losses are often inflated by SBC charges that do not recur at the same magnitude. The rapid swing to profitability in FY2025 confirms that operating profitability was closer than the headline loss suggested.

### Operating Leverage

The dramatic profitability improvement on 69.4% revenue growth demonstrates significant operating leverage in Reddit's business model. Key drivers:

- **Platform economics:** Marginal cost of serving additional users and ads is near-zero.
- **Data licensing:** Extremely high-margin revenue — the data already exists; licensing it requires minimal incremental cost.
- **Moderator model:** Reddit's community moderation is largely volunteer-driven, reducing content moderation costs relative to peers.
- **Capital-light infrastructure:** $6.7M in CapEx suggests Reddit relies heavily on cloud infrastructure (variable cost) rather than owned data centers.

| **FCF Yield** | 2.3% | $684M / $30.3B market cap |
| **FCF Margin** | 31.1% | $684M / $2,203M revenue |

### Cash Flow Analysis

**Capital-light excellence:** Reddit's $6.7M annual CapEx on $2.2B in revenue is remarkable. At 0.3% of revenue, this is among the lowest capital intensity ratios of any publicly traded technology company. For context, Meta spends ~25% of revenue on CapEx (data centers for AI), and even "capital-light" SaaS companies typically spend 3-8% of revenue on CapEx. Reddit's model converts nearly 100% of operating cash flow into free cash flow.

**Why so little CapEx?** Reddit likely relies on:
- Cloud infrastructure providers (AWS, GCP) rather than building owned data centers
- Volunteer community moderators rather than large-scale content moderation operations
- A text-and-image-heavy platform that requires less compute than video-first platforms

**FCF/NI ratio of 129%:** A ratio above 100% indicates that free cash flow exceeds reported net income — a hallmark of high earnings quality. This means Reddit's reported profits are backed by real cash generation, not accounting artifacts. The premium likely reflects depreciation/amortization charges and working capital timing benefits.

**Cash generation trajectory:** With Q1 and Q2 2026 EPS of $1.01 and $1.25 respectively (implying continued profitability growth), FCF is likely continuing to expand in 2026. An annualized FCF estimate for FY2026 could reasonably exceed $1B.

### Free Cash Flow Uses

With $684M+ in annual FCF and minimal capital requirements, Reddit has significant optionality:
- Share buybacks to return capital and offset SBC dilution
- Strategic acquisitions to expand the platform ecosystem
- Investment in AI capabilities and data products
- Building a cash reserve for durability through advertising cycles

### Valuation Deep Dive

**The PEG story is the headline.** Reddit's forward P/E of 16.3x against 60%+ revenue growth produces a PEG ratio of approximately 0.27. To put this in context:

| PEG Range | Interpretation | RDDT's Position |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| < 0.5 | Potentially deeply undervalued | **RDDT at ~0.27** |
| 0.5 - 1.0 | Attractive | |
| 1.0 | Fair value (Peter Lynch rule) | |
| 1.0 - 2.0 | Moderately expensive | |
| > 2.0 | Expensive | |

A PEG of 0.27 means investors are paying just 27 cents for each percentage point of growth. This is rare for a profitable, large-cap company with positive FCF. The market is either (a) pricing in significant growth deceleration, (b) discounting the sustainability of current margins, or (c) applying a risk premium for the stock's extreme volatility and short public history.

**Trailing vs. Forward P/E gap:** The spread from 37.2x trailing to 16.3x forward is dramatic and reflects rapid earnings growth. This gap will narrow as trailing earnings catch up to the forward trajectory. If FY2026 EPS comes in at ~$4.50-$5.00 (implied by Q1-Q2 2026 quarterly EPS), the trailing P/E would compress to roughly 10-11x at the current price — which would be remarkably cheap for a 60% grower.

**EV/Sales of 10.1x:** This is a premium multiple, but it must be contextualized against Reddit's 31% FCF margin. A company converting 31 cents of every revenue dollar into free cash flow can support a higher revenue multiple. EV/Sales / FCF Margin = 10.1 / 0.31 = 32.6x FCF, which is reasonable for a high-growth company.

**EV/EBITDA of 34.9x:** Elevated on a trailing basis but compressing rapidly. If EBITDA grows at even half the rate of revenue (30-35%), this multiple would compress to ~25x within a year without any stock price appreciation.

### Implied Growth Expectations

Using reverse DCF logic, the current valuation implies the market expects:
- Revenue growth to decelerate to ~25-30% within 2-3 years
- Long-term FCF margins of 25-30%
- A terminal growth rate of 3-4%

If Reddit can sustain 40%+ growth for longer than the market expects, or if margins expand beyond 30%, there is meaningful upside from current levels.

### Risk Analysis

**Volatility context:** An annualized volatility of 79.7% means that, statistically, RDDT's price could move roughly +/- 80% in a year with normal probability. This is approximately 4-5x the volatility of the S&P 500 (~15-17%). Investors must size positions accordingly.

**Max drawdown of -85.5%:** This is an extreme figure. Since its IPO in March 2024 through the 622 available trading days, RDDT has experienced an 85.5% peak-to-trough decline at some point. This means an investor who bought at the peak would have lost over 85 cents on every dollar invested. While the stock has recovered (current price: $50.44), this drawdown history illustrates the extreme risk of timing.

**VaR 95% of -7.6% daily:** On any given trading day, there is a 5% chance (roughly once per month) that RDDT could decline 7.6% or more. On a $10,000 position, this translates to a $760+ loss in a single day with 5% probability.

### Risk Factors (Qualitative)

1. **Advertising cyclicality:** Reddit's dominant revenue source is advertising, which is cyclical. An economic slowdown could reduce ad spending and compress growth.

2. **Growth deceleration risk:** The market's growth expectations are critical. Any quarter where growth drops significantly below 50% could trigger a sharp re-rating lower.

3. **Content moderation and regulatory risk:** As a platform hosting user-generated content, Reddit faces ongoing regulatory scrutiny (Section 230 reform, EU Digital Services Act), content moderation costs, and reputational risk from problematic content.

4. **AI data licensing durability:** The data licensing revenue stream depends on continued AI industry demand. Changes in AI training methodologies (synthetic data, smaller models) could reduce demand for Reddit's data.

5. **Competition for user attention:** Reddit competes with TikTok, YouTube, Twitter/X, Discord, and emerging platforms for user time and engagement.

6. **Short public history:** With only ~2.5 years as a public company, Reddit has limited track record for investors to evaluate management execution, capital allocation, and communication quality.

7. **Stock-based compensation:** As a recently-public tech company, SBC may be significant and could dilute shareholders. The gap between GAAP and non-GAAP profitability should be monitored.

**Risk score: 3/10 (Below Average)** — exceptional fundamentals offset by extreme price volatility and the qualitative risks of a young public company in a cyclical advertising market.

### 1. Valuation Metrics

**P/E (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)**
What the market pays per dollar of earnings. Higher = more expensive relative to current profits.
> P/E = Stock Price / EPS = $100 / $5.00 = **20.0x**
> Interpretation: investors pay $20 for every $1 of Acme's annual profit.

**Forward P/E**
P/E using *next year's estimated* earnings instead of trailing. Lower than trailing P/E when earnings are expected to grow.
> If analysts estimate next year EPS = $6.50:
> Forward P/E = $100 / $6.50 = **15.4x**

**PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings-to-Growth)**
P/E divided by expected EPS growth rate. Adjusts valuation for growth speed. PEG < 1 may suggest undervaluation.
> If EPS growth rate = 25%:
> PEG = 20.0 / 25 = **0.80**
> Interpretation: paying less than 1x the growth rate — potentially attractive.

**EV (Enterprise Value)**
Total company value including debt, minus cash. What an acquirer would pay to buy the whole business.
> EV = Market Cap + Total Debt - Cash
> EV = ($100 x 10M) + $200M - $50M = $1,000M + $200M - $50M = **$1,150M**

**EV/EBITDA**
Enterprise value per dollar of operating cash earnings. Lower = cheaper. Useful for comparing companies with different debt levels.
> EV/EBITDA = $1,150M / $90M = **12.8x**
> Interpretation: the business is valued at 12.8 years of its operating cash earnings.

**EV/Sales (EV/Revenue)**
Enterprise value per dollar of revenue. Used for high-growth companies that may not yet be profitable.
> EV/Sales = $1,150M / $500M = **2.3x**

**P/S (Price-to-Sales)**
Market cap per dollar of revenue. Simpler than EV/Sales (ignores debt/cash).
> P/S = Market Cap / Revenue = $1,000M / $500M = **2.0x**

**P/B (Price-to-Book)**
Market price relative to accounting book value. P/B < 1 means market values the company below its asset base.
> P/B = Stock Price / Book Value per Share = $100 / $30 = **3.3x**

**FCF Yield (Free Cash Flow Yield)**
Free cash flow as a percentage of market cap. Higher = more cash generation per dollar of market value. Inverse of P/FCF.
> FCF Yield = FCF / Market Cap = $40M / $1,000M = **4.0%**
> Interpretation: Acme generates 4 cents of free cash for every $1 of market value annually.

**Dividend Yield**
Annual dividends as a percentage of stock price.
> Dividend Yield = Dividends/Share / Price = $1.50 / $100 = **1.5%**

---

### 2. Profitability Metrics

**Gross Margin**
Revenue remaining after direct production costs. Measures pricing power and production efficiency.
> Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue = $200M / $500M = **40.0%**
> Interpretation: Acme keeps 40 cents of every revenue dollar after paying for what it sells.

**Operating Margin**
Revenue remaining after all operating expenses (but before interest and taxes). Core business profitability.
> Operating Margin = Operating Income / Revenue = $75M / $500M = **15.0%**

**Net Margin**
Bottom-line profit per dollar of revenue, after all costs including taxes and interest.
> Net Margin = Net Income / Revenue = $50M / $500M = **10.0%**

**ROE (Return on Equity)**
Profit generated per dollar of shareholder equity. Measures how efficiently the company uses shareholder capital.
> ROE = Net Income / Total Equity = $50M / $300M = **16.7%**
> Interpretation: for every $1 of equity, Acme generates 16.7 cents of annual profit.

**ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)**
Profit generated per dollar of ALL capital deployed (equity + debt). Better than ROE for comparing companies with different leverage.
> NOPAT = Operating Income x (1 - Tax Rate) = $75M x 0.75 = $56.25M
> ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital = $56.25M / $450M = **12.5%**
> Interpretation: Acme earns 12.5% on every dollar of capital invested. Compare to WACC (10%) — if ROIC > WACC, the company creates value.

---

### 3. Cash Flow Metrics

**OCF (Operating Cash Flow)**
Cash generated from normal business operations. Starts from net income, adds back non-cash charges, adjusts for working capital changes.
> OCF = $70M (from cash flow statement)

**FCF (Free Cash Flow)**
Cash available after maintaining/expanding the asset base. The cash that could be returned to shareholders or used for growth.
> FCF = Operating Cash Flow - CapEx = $70M - $30M = **$40M**

**CapEx (Capital Expenditures)**
Spending on physical assets (factories, equipment, data centers). A measure of investment intensity.
> CapEx = $30M
> CapEx/Revenue = $30M / $500M = 6.0% (capital-light if <10%, capital-heavy if >20%)

**FCF/NI (FCF-to-Net-Income Ratio)**
Cash quality indicator. FCF/NI > 100% means the company generates more cash than reported profit — high earnings quality. FCF/NI < 80% may signal aggressive accounting.
> FCF/NI = $40M / $50M = **80%**

---

### 4. Growth Metrics

**TTM (Trailing Twelve Months)**
Sum of the last four quarters. Used to annualize quarterly data without calendar-year bias.
> TTM Revenue = Q3 + Q4 + Q1 + Q2 = $120M + $130M + $125M + $125M = **$500M**

**YoY (Year-over-Year)**
Growth rate comparing the same period to the prior year. Removes seasonality.
> If Q2 2026 revenue = $125M and Q2 2025 revenue = $100M:
> YoY Growth = ($125M - $100M) / $100M = **25.0%**

**CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)**
Smoothed annualized growth rate over multiple years. Accounts for compounding.
> If revenue was $200M five years ago and is $500M now:
> CAGR = ($500M/$200M)^(1/5) - 1 = (2.5)^0.2 - 1 = **20.1%**
> Interpretation: revenue grew at an average of 20.1% per year, compounded.

**EPS (Earnings Per Share)**
Net income allocated to each share. The denominator for P/E.
> EPS = Net Income / Shares Outstanding = $50M / 10M = **$5.00**

---

### 5. Risk Metrics

**Annualized Volatility**
Standard deviation of daily returns, annualized. Higher = more price uncertainty. Typical ranges: <15% low, 15-25% medium, 25-40% high, >40% very high.
> If daily return std dev = 2.0%:
> Annualized Vol = 2.0% x sqrt(252) = 2.0% x 15.87 = **31.7%** (high)

**Max Drawdown**
Largest peak-to-trough decline in stock price. Measures worst-case historical loss.
> If stock peaked at $150 and bottomed at $60:
> Max Drawdown = ($60 - $150) / $150 = **-60%**

**VaR 95% (Value at Risk, 95th percentile)**
The daily loss that is exceeded only 5% of trading days. A "bad day" threshold.
> If 5th percentile of daily returns = -3.2%:
> VaR 95% = **-3.2%** (on 95% of days, you lose less than 3.2%)

**Beta**
Stock's sensitivity to market movements. Beta 1.0 = moves with market. Beta 1.5 = 50% more volatile than market. Beta 0.7 = 30% less volatile.
> If Acme's beta = 1.3:
> When S&P 500 falls 10%, Acme is expected to fall ~13%.

**Debt/Equity**
Total debt relative to shareholder equity. Measures financial leverage.
> D/E = Total Debt / Total Equity = $200M / $300M = **0.67**
> Interpretation: for every $1 of equity, Acme has $0.67 of debt. Under 1.0 is generally conservative.

---

### 6. Valuation Models (used in this report)

**Owner-Earnings Value (Buffett method)**
Intrinsic value based on sustainable cash earnings. Conservative — applies a 25% margin of safety haircut.
> Owner Earnings = Net Income + Depreciation - Maintenance CapEx
> Value = Owner Earnings x Growth Multiplier x (1 - 25% MoS)

**DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)**
Projects future free cash flows and discounts them to present value. The "gold standard" of intrinsic value — but only as good as the assumptions.
> Value = Sum(FCF_t / (1 + WACC)^t) + Terminal Value
> Run three scenarios: Bear (low growth), Base (consensus), Bull (high growth)

**Graham Number**
Benjamin Graham's conservative intrinsic value formula. Suitable for stable, profitable companies.
> Graham Number = sqrt(22.5 x EPS x Book Value per Share)
> = sqrt(22.5 x $5.00 x $30) = sqrt($3,375) = **$58.09**
> Interpretation: Graham would pay no more than $58.09 for Acme.

**EV/EBITDA Relative Value**
Per-share value implied by applying the sector median EV/EBITDA multiple.
> If sector median EV/EBITDA = 10x:
> Implied EV = 10 x $90M = $900M
> Implied Equity = $900M - $200M + $50M = $750M
> Implied Price = $750M / 10M shares = **$75.00**

**PEG-Based Fair Value**
Fair P/E = EPS growth rate (Peter Lynch rule of thumb). PEG = 1 means "fair."
> If growth = 25%, fair P/E = 25x
> Fair Price = 25 x $5.00 = **$125.00**

1. Valuation Metrics

P/E (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)
What the market pays per dollar of earnings. Higher = more expensive relative to current profits.

P/E = Stock Price / EPS = $287 / $15.86 = 18.1x
Interpretation: investors pay $18.10 for every $1 of EXPE's annual profit.

Forward P/E
P/E using next year's estimated earnings instead of trailing. Lower than trailing P/E when earnings are expected to grow.

Analysts estimate next-year EPS = $24.50:
Forward P/E = $287 / $24.50 = 11.7x
The drop from 18.1x trailing to 11.7x forward implies analysts expect ~55% earnings growth.

PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings-to-Growth)
P/E divided by expected EPS growth rate. Adjusts valuation for growth speed. PEG < 1 may suggest undervaluation.

Forward EPS growth rate = ($24.50 - $15.86) / $15.86 = 54.5%
PEG = 18.1 / 54.5 = 0.33
Interpretation: paying well below 1x the growth rate -- potentially very attractive if the growth estimate holds.

EV (Enterprise Value)
Total company value including debt, minus cash. What an acquirer would pay to buy the whole business.

EV = Market Cap + Total Debt - Cash
EV = $34.4B + $8.7B - $3.7B = $39.4B

EV/EBITDA
Enterprise value per dollar of operating cash earnings. Lower = cheaper. Useful for comparing companies with different debt levels.

EV/EBITDA = $39.4B / $2.89B = 13.6x
Interpretation: the business is valued at 13.6 years of its operating cash earnings.

EV/Sales (EV/Revenue)
Enterprise value per dollar of revenue. Used for high-growth companies that may not yet be profitable.

EV/Sales = $39.4B / $14.73B = 2.7x

P/S (Price-to-Sales)
Market cap per dollar of revenue. Simpler than EV/Sales (ignores debt/cash).

P/S = Market Cap / Revenue = $34.4B / $14.73B = 2.3x

P/B (Price-to-Book)
Market price relative to accounting book value. P/B < 1 means market values the company below its asset base.

P/B = Stock Price / Book Value per Share = $287 / $31.70 = 9.1x
EXPE's high P/B reflects intangible-heavy travel tech -- brand value and platform network effects do not appear on the balance sheet.

FCF Yield (Free Cash Flow Yield)
Free cash flow as a percentage of market cap. Higher = more cash generation per dollar of market value. Inverse of P/FCF.

FCF Yield = FCF / Market Cap = $3.11B / $34.4B = 9.0%
Interpretation: EXPE generates 9 cents of free cash for every $1 of market value annually -- among the highest in this batch.

Dividend Yield
Annual dividends as a percentage of stock price.

EXPE does not currently pay a dividend, so Dividend Yield = $0 / $287 = 0.0%
For context: if EXPE were to pay out 20% of FCF (~$622M, or ~$5.18/share), the yield would be $5.18 / $287 = 1.8%.

2. Profitability Metrics

Gross Margin
Revenue remaining after direct production costs. Measures pricing power and production efficiency.

Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue = $5.60B / $14.73B = 38.0%
Interpretation: EXPE keeps 38 cents of every revenue dollar after paying for the services it delivers.

Operating Margin
Revenue remaining after all operating expenses (but before interest and taxes). Core business profitability.

Operating Margin = Operating Income / Revenue = $2.21B / $14.73B = 15.0%

Net Margin
Bottom-line profit per dollar of revenue, after all costs including taxes and interest.

Net Margin = Net Income / Revenue = $1.86B / $14.73B = 12.6%

ROE (Return on Equity)
Profit generated per dollar of shareholder equity. Measures how efficiently the company uses shareholder capital.

ROE = Net Income / Total Equity = $1.86B / $3.80B = 49.0%
Interpretation: for every $1 of equity, EXPE generates 49 cents of annual profit. This extremely high ROE is partly driven by leverage (D/E = 2.3) -- debt amplifies equity returns.

ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)
Profit generated per dollar of ALL capital deployed (equity + debt). Better than ROE for comparing companies with different leverage.

NOPAT = Operating Income x (1 - Effective Tax Rate) = $2.21B x 0.85 = $1.88B
ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital = $1.88B / $12.5B = 15.0%
Interpretation: EXPE earns 15.0% on every dollar of capital invested. Compare to WACC (10%) -- ROIC > WACC means the company creates value.

3. Cash Flow Metrics

OCF (Operating Cash Flow)
Cash generated from normal business operations. Starts from net income, adds back non-cash charges, adjusts for working capital changes.

OCF = $4.35B (from cash flow statement)

FCF (Free Cash Flow)
Cash available after maintaining/expanding the asset base. The cash that could be returned to shareholders or used for growth.

FCF = Operating Cash Flow - CapEx = $4.35B - $1.24B = $3.11B

CapEx (Capital Expenditures)
Spending on physical assets (factories, equipment, data centers). A measure of investment intensity.

CapEx = $1.24B
CapEx/Revenue = $1.24B / $14.73B = 8.4% (capital-light if <10%, capital-heavy if >20%)
EXPE is capital-light -- most spending is on technology, not physical infrastructure.

FCF/NI (FCF-to-Net-Income Ratio)
Cash quality indicator. FCF/NI > 100% means the company generates more cash than reported profit -- high earnings quality. FCF/NI < 80% may signal aggressive accounting.

FCF/NI = $3.11B / $1.86B = 167%
Interpretation: EXPE's cash generation significantly exceeds reported net income -- a strong earnings-quality signal. The gap is driven by depreciation/amortization add-backs exceeding CapEx.

4. Growth Metrics

TTM (Trailing Twelve Months)
Sum of the last four quarters. Used to annualize quarterly data without calendar-year bias.

TTM Revenue = Q3 + Q4 + Q1 + Q2 = $3.56B + $3.89B + $3.52B + $3.76B = $14.73B

YoY (Year-over-Year)
Growth rate comparing the same period to the prior year. Removes seasonality.

If Q2 2026 revenue = $3.76B and Q2 2025 revenue = $3.32B:
YoY Growth = ($3.76B - $3.32B) / $3.32B = 13.3%

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)
Smoothed annualized growth rate over multiple years. Accounts for compounding.

If EXPE's revenue was $8.60B five years ago and is $14.73B now:
CAGR = ($14.73B / $8.60B)^(1/5) - 1 = (1.713)^0.2 - 1 = 11.4%
Interpretation: revenue grew at an average of 11.4% per year, compounded.

EPS (Earnings Per Share)
Net income allocated to each share. The denominator for P/E.

EPS = Net Income / Shares Outstanding = $1.86B / ~120M = $15.86

5. Risk Metrics

Annualized Volatility
Standard deviation of daily returns, annualized. Higher = more price uncertainty. Typical ranges: <15% low, 15-25% medium, 25-40% high, >40% very high.

EXPE daily return std dev = ~2.82%:
Annualized Vol = 2.82% x sqrt(252) = 2.82% x 15.87 = 44.7% (very high)
EXPE sits at the top of the "medium risk" tier in this batch -- reflecting travel-sector cyclicality.

Max Drawdown
Largest peak-to-trough decline in stock price. Measures worst-case historical loss.

EXPE's historical max drawdown = -86.7%
This occurred during the COVID-19 pandemic when travel bookings collapsed. It demonstrates the extreme tail risk in travel stocks during black-swan events.

VaR 95% (Value at Risk, 95th percentile)
The daily loss that is exceeded only 5% of trading days. A "bad day" threshold.

EXPE's 5th percentile of daily returns = -4.0%
On 95% of trading days, EXPE loses less than 4.0%. On a $287 position, that is ~$11.48 per share.

Beta
Stock's sensitivity to market movements. Beta 1.0 = moves with market. Beta 1.5 = 50% more volatile than market. Beta 0.7 = 30% less volatile.

EXPE's beta = 1.3:
When the S&P 500 falls 10%, EXPE is expected to fall ~13%.
When the S&P 500 rises 10%, EXPE is expected to rise ~13%.

Debt/Equity
Total debt relative to shareholder equity. Measures financial leverage.

D/E = Total Debt / Total Equity = $8.7B / $3.80B = 2.3
Interpretation: for every $1 of equity, EXPE has $2.30 of debt. This is elevated (>1.0) and reflects the travel industry's capital structure -- EXPE uses debt to fund acquisitions and buybacks. The high leverage amplifies both ROE (49%) and downside risk.

6. Valuation Models (used in this report)

Owner-Earnings Value (Buffett method)
Intrinsic value based on sustainable cash earnings. Conservative -- applies a 25% margin of safety haircut.

Owner Earnings = Net Income + Depreciation - Maintenance CapEx
Value = Owner Earnings x Growth Multiplier x (1 - 25% MoS)

DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)
Projects future free cash flows and discounts them to present value. The "gold standard" of intrinsic value -- but only as good as the assumptions.

Value = SUM(FCF_t / (1 + WACC)^t) + Terminal Value

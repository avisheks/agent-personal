## 3. Company Fundamentals

### Business Overview

Tesla designs, manufactures, and sells electric vehicles, energy storage systems, and solar products. The company is also developing autonomous driving technology and humanoid robots (Optimus). **[FACT/INFERENCE]**

### Revenue Streams

1. **Automotive (~80% of revenue):** Vehicle sales, leasing, regulatory credits, and services. **[INFERENCE]**
2. **Energy Generation & Storage (~10-12%):** Megapack utility storage, Powerwall home storage, and solar products. Fastest-growing segment. **[INFERENCE]**
3. **Services & Other (~8-10%):** Supercharging, insurance, body shop, and merchandise. **[INFERENCE]**

### Competitive Moats

- **Brand and cult following:** Tesla has one of the strongest consumer brands in the world, though increasingly polarized by CEO Elon Musk's political activities. **[INFERENCE]**
- **Supercharger network:** The largest and most reliable EV charging network, now adopted as an industry standard (NACS). **[INFERENCE]**
- **Vertical integration:** In-house manufacturing of batteries, software, and hardware provides cost and quality control advantages. **[INFERENCE]**
- **Data advantage for autonomy:** Largest fleet of camera-equipped vehicles generating real-world driving data for FSD development. **[INFERENCE]**
- **Energy storage leadership:** Megapack is the leading utility-scale battery storage product globally. **[INFERENCE]**

## 11. Contrarian Analysis

### The Bull Case

1. **Robotaxi is transformative.** If Tesla achieves Level 4/5 autonomy, the revenue opportunity is $100B+ annually from a software/service model with 70%+ margins. This alone justifies the current market cap. **[INFERENCE]**
2. **Energy storage is exploding.** Megapack demand is growing 100%+ YoY and could become a $20-50B revenue business by 2030. **[INFERENCE]**
3. **Revenue recovery in Q2 2026 (+25.5%) shows the auto business isn't dead.** The FY2025 decline was a trough, not a trend. **[FACT/INFERENCE]**
4. **Optimus robot is underpriced optionality.** If humanoid robots achieve commercial viability, the addressable market dwarfs automotive. **[INFERENCE]**

### The Bear Case

1. **345x trailing P/E for a declining-profit company is indefensible.** No growth story justifies paying 345x current earnings when those earnings are falling. **[FACT/INFERENCE]**
2. **Robotaxi is perpetually "next year."** Full self-driving has been promised since 2016 and remains Level 2 in practice. **[INFERENCE]**
3. **Brand damage from CEO.** Musk's political activities are measurably impacting Tesla's brand perception and sales in key markets (Europe, blue-state US). **[INFERENCE]**
4. **Chinese competition is existential.** BYD is now outselling Tesla globally at lower price points with comparable technology. **[INFERENCE]**
5. **The 97.6% max drawdown shows this stock can lose nearly everything.** **[FACT]**

### Contrarian Verdict

TSLA is not an investment in the current business -- it is a venture-stage bet on robotaxi, energy, and AI at public market scale. The auto fundamentals do not justify the valuation by any traditional metric. Investors must have extreme conviction in Tesla's technology roadmap to hold at these prices. **[INFERENCE]**

## 12. Things the Market May Be Missing

### Underappreciated Positives

1. **Energy storage could be a standalone $100B+ company.** Megapack growth is accelerating and margins are high. The market may be undervaluing this segment within the consolidated financials. **[INFERENCE]**
2. **Manufacturing cost leadership.** Despite price cuts, Tesla's manufacturing efficiency (especially at Giga Shanghai) provides cost advantages that competitors cannot easily replicate. **[INFERENCE]**
3. **Supercharger network monetization.** As the NACS standard, Tesla's charging network could generate significant revenue from non-Tesla EVs. **[INFERENCE]**

### Underappreciated Risks

1. **Concentration risk in Elon Musk.** No other company of this size is so dependent on a single individual who is simultaneously CEO of multiple other companies. **[INFERENCE]**
2. **China risk.** A significant portion of Tesla's production and sales is in China, exposing it to geopolitical tensions and local competitive dynamics. **[INFERENCE]**
3. **Capital allocation questions.** Heavy investment in Optimus and AI while auto margins compress raises questions about capital discipline. **[INFERENCE]**
4. **Insurance and liability for FSD.** As autonomy features expand, Tesla faces growing liability risk from accidents involving its technology. **[INFERENCE]**

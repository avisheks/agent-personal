## 11. Contrarian Analysis

**What the consensus believes:** Intuit is a disruption victim. AI-powered tax prep (ChatGPT, various startups), IRS Direct File, and AI accounting tools will erode TurboTax and QuickBooks, Intuit's two largest revenue streams. The 60% stock decline is the market correctly pricing in future earnings deterioration. [INFERENCE: derived from price action and public commentary]

**What the contrarian sees:**

1. **The business is not deteriorating.** Revenue grew 13.9% in FY2026, 15.6% in FY2025, 13.3% in FY2024. FCF margins expanded from 29% to 40%. Net income growth has outpaced revenue growth every year. [FACT: research packet]

2. **AI is more likely a tailwind than a headwind for Intuit.** Intuit has $21B in revenue to invest in AI capabilities. It owns the data (tax returns, business financials, credit data, marketing data) that makes AI useful. Startups building "AI tax prep" from scratch lack the regulatory compliance infrastructure, data moats, and distribution. [INFERENCE]

3. **IRS Direct File has been a persistent non-event.** The IRS has struggled to scale Direct File beyond simple returns. The complexity of the US tax code (state taxes, deductions, self-employment, investment income) makes a government tool structurally limited. [INFERENCE]

4. **QuickBooks switching costs are underestimated.** Small businesses that run payroll, invoicing, and tax filings through QuickBooks face enormous friction switching to an alternative. This is not a consumer app -- it is embedded infrastructure. [INFERENCE]

5. **The valuation has overshot on the downside.** At 10.2x forward earnings and 11.7% FCF yield, Intuit is priced like a legacy business in secular decline (think IBM circa 2015). The actual growth rate is 14%. [FACT/INFERENCE]

**Contrarian probability assessment:** 60% probability the consensus is wrong and INTU re-rates higher within 18 months. 25% probability the market is early but ultimately correct that growth decelerates to <5%. 15% probability of genuine disruption that impairs the business model. [FORECAST]

## 12. Things the Market May Be Missing

1. **Intuit's AI integration advantage.** Intuit Assist (AI-powered financial assistant) is embedded across TurboTax, QuickBooks, Credit Karma, and Mailchimp. Rather than disrupting Intuit, AI is being deployed to deepen customer engagement and increase ARPU. The market may be applying a generic "AI disrupts incumbents" narrative without considering that Intuit is itself an AI deployer with proprietary data. [INFERENCE]

2. **FCF margin expansion trajectory.** FCF margins expanded from 29% (FY2022) to 40% (FY2026) -- an 1,100bps improvement in four years. If this trajectory continues (even at a slower pace), FY2028 FCF could reach $10-12B, making the current $74B EV look deeply discounted. [FORECAST]

3. **Small business formation tailwind.** New business applications in the US remain elevated post-pandemic. Each new business is a potential QuickBooks customer. This secular tailwind is not reflected in the disruption-focused narrative. [INFERENCE]

4. **Tax code complexity is increasing, not decreasing.** Cryptocurrency reporting, gig economy income, SALT cap changes, and potential tax reform all increase the complexity that drives demand for TurboTax. AI makes simple returns easier but complex returns harder -- and complexity is where Intuit monetizes. [INFERENCE]

5. **Buyback firepower.** At $8.66B annual FCF and a $73.7B market cap, Intuit could retire ~12% of its float annually through buybacks alone. Even modest capital allocation toward repurchases would provide significant EPS accretion at these depressed share prices. [INFERENCE]

## 12. Things the Market May Be Missing

1. **FCF Power is Accelerating, Not Decelerating** [FACT] FCF grew from $4.69B (FY2024) to $6.12B (FY2025) to $8.66B (FY2026) -- a 36% CAGR over two years. FCF margins expanded from 28.8% to 40.4%. The market is pricing in stagnation while the cash generation machine is actually improving. At $79.8B market cap, the stock yields nearly 11% on free cash flow.

2. **AI as Tailwind, Not Headwind** [INFERENCE] Intuit has invested heavily in "Intuit Assist" (AI-powered features across all platforms). For a company with 100M+ users generating financial data, AI enables: (a) reduced customer support costs, (b) better upsell/cross-sell recommendations, (c) automated bookkeeping that increases QuickBooks stickiness, (d) faster tax preparation that improves NPS. Companies with proprietary data and existing distribution tend to benefit from AI, not be disrupted by it.

3. **Buyback Capacity at Depressed Prices** [INFERENCE] With $8.66B in annual FCF and modest net debt (~$2.4B implied from EV minus market cap), Intuit has enormous capacity to repurchase shares at historically cheap levels. At $299/share, $4B in annual buybacks would retire ~5% of shares outstanding, providing a significant EPS tailwind even with flat earnings.

4. **Tax Complexity is Increasing, Not Decreasing** [INFERENCE] New tax legislation (SALT cap changes, cryptocurrency reporting requirements, gig economy 1099 rules) adds complexity that drives demand for tax software. IRS Direct File is designed for simple returns; as the tax code gets more complex, more filers need TurboTax, not fewer.

5. **QuickBooks Online Ecosystem Lock-In** [INFERENCE] QuickBooks is not just accounting software -- it's a platform connecting payroll, payments, banking, time tracking, and inventory for 7M+ small businesses. Switching costs are enormous because it means migrating years of financial history and retraining staff. This moat is underappreciated.

## Things the Market May Be Missing

### Potentially Underappreciated Factors

1. **OCF inflection point:** The 887% OCF growth from Q2 2025 to Q2 2026 suggests the core business (ex-CapEx) is already cash generative at scale. **[FACT]** If CapEx normalizes to $10-15B/quarter within 12-18 months, SPCX could achieve positive FCF far sooner than the market expects. **[INFERENCE]** This would fundamentally re-rate the stock upward. **[FORECAST]**

2. **Starship economics are not priced in:** Current revenue is dominated by Falcon 9 launches and Starlink subscriptions. **[INFERENCE]** Starship, once operational, could reduce cost-per-kg-to-orbit by 90%+, opening entirely new commercial markets. **[INFERENCE]** The market may be valuing Starship as a CapEx burden rather than a revenue catalyst. **[INFERENCE]**

3. **Government defense spending tailwind:** Rising geopolitical tensions are driving increased defense and intelligence spending on space capabilities. SpaceX is uniquely positioned as the only commercial provider with demonstrated rapid-launch capability. **[INFERENCE]**

4. **Starlink Direct-to-Cell:** The partnership model for satellite-to-smartphone connectivity could add billions in high-margin revenue with minimal incremental CapEx. This is not well-understood by generalist investors. **[INFERENCE]**

### Potentially Overappreciated Factors

1. **Mars mission timeline:** Some of the valuation premium appears to incorporate long-term Mars colonization revenue, which is decades away and highly uncertain. **[INFERENCE]**

2. **Subscriber growth extrapolation:** Straight-line extrapolation of Starlink subscriber growth ignores the difference between easy-to-reach markets (rural broadband in developed countries) and harder markets (dense urban, developing nations). **[INFERENCE]**

3. **Monopoly assumptions:** The market may underestimate the competitive response from Amazon Kuiper (backed by AWS cash flows), Telesat, and government-funded programs. **[INFERENCE]**

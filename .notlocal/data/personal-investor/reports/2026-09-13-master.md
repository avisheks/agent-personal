### Worked Examples: Valuation

#### P/E (Price-to-Earnings)

What the market pays per dollar of current earnings.

```
P/E = Stock Price / EPS
    = $294 / $9.81
    = 30.0x
```

Wait -- the report states 18.5x trailing P/E. The difference arises because the report uses a price near $181 at the time of the data snapshot (Market Cap $34.4B / 117M shares = ~$294 current price, but the P/E was computed at an earlier price point against annual EPS). Using the report's stated values:

```
Trailing P/E (report) = 18.5x
Interpretation: investors pay $18.50 for every $1 of EXPE's trailing annual profit.
```

The 18.5x P/E at EPS $9.81 implies the price used was ~$181.5. Current price of $294 would give a trailing P/E of ~30x -- meaning the stock has re-rated significantly.

#### Forward P/E

Uses next-year analyst estimates instead of trailing earnings.

```
Forward P/E = Stock Price / Forward EPS Estimate
Report states: 11.7x
At current price $294: Forward EPS = $294 / 11.7 = ~$25.13
```

The 37% gap between trailing P/E (18.5x) and forward P/E (11.7x) implies analysts expect EPS to grow from $9.81 to ~$25.13 -- a 156% increase. This unusually large gap is flagged in the report as a potential data anomaly or aggressive estimate.

#### Enterprise Value (EV)

Total value of the business including debt, minus cash. What an acquirer would pay.

```
EV = Market Cap + Total Debt - Cash
   = $34.4B + $5.6B - $5.7B
   = $34.3B
```

EV approximately equals Market Cap because net debt is near zero (-$0.1B). This means EXPE's debt is almost entirely offset by its cash holdings.

#### EV/EBITDA

Enterprise value per dollar of operating cash earnings. The primary valuation metric for the travel sector.

```
EV/EBITDA = Enterprise Value / EBITDA
          = $34.3B / $2.88B
          = 11.9x

Interpretation: the business is valued at 11.9 years of operating cash earnings.
```

#### EV/Sales

```
EV/Sales = Enterprise Value / Revenue (TTM)
         = $34.3B / $14.7B
         = 2.33x (report rounds to 2.2x)
```

#### P/S (Price-to-Sales)

```
P/S = Market Cap / Revenue (TTM)
    = $34.4B / $14.7B
    = 2.34x (report rounds to 2.2x)
```

P/S and EV/Sales are nearly identical for EXPE because net debt is approximately zero.

#### FCF Yield

Free cash flow as a percentage of market cap. Higher is better -- it measures cash return on market price.

```
FCF Yield = FCF / Market Cap
          = $3.11B / $34.4B
          = 9.04%

Interpretation: EXPE generates ~9 cents of free cash for every $1 of market value.
The S&P 500 average FCF yield is roughly 4-5%, so EXPE's 9% is nearly double.
```

### Worked Examples: Valuation Models

#### Reverse DCF -- What Growth Is the Market Pricing In?

The reverse DCF works backward from the current price to solve for the implied growth rate.

**Step 1: Terminal value at zero growth**

```
Terminal Value = FCF / (WACC - Terminal Growth)
At WACC 10%, terminal growth 3%:
  = $3.11B / (0.10 - 0.03)
  = $3.11B / 0.07
  = $44.4B
```

**Step 2: Compare to current EV**

```
Current EV: $34.3B
Zero-growth perpetuity value: $44.4B
Discount: ($34.3B - $44.4B) / $44.4B = -22.7%
```

**Step 3: Interpret**

The market is pricing EXPE at a ~23% discount to what its current FCF would be worth even with zero growth. This implies the market expects FCF to DECLINE from $3.11B, or alternatively demands a higher return (WACC > 10%).

**Alternative at WACC 12%:**

```
Terminal Value = $3.11B / (0.12 - 0.03) = $3.11B / 0.09 = $34.6B
This approximately equals the current EV of $34.3B.
Implication: the market demands a 12% return and prices in zero real growth.
```

#### Forward DCF -- Three Scenarios

Common assumptions: WACC = 10%, terminal growth = 3%, 10-year projection.

**Bear Case: FCF shrinks 2%/year for Y1-5, then flat**

```
Year 0 FCF: $3.11B
Year 5 FCF: $3.11B x (0.98)^5 = $3.11B x 0.904 = $2.81B
Year 10 FCF: $2.81B (flat Y6-10)
Terminal value at Y10: $2.81B / (0.10 - 0.03) = $40.1B
PV of terminal: $40.1B / (1.10)^10 = $40.1B / 2.594 = $15.5B
PV of interim FCFs: ~$13.0B (sum of discounted Y1-10 cash flows)
Implied EV: ~$28.5B
Implied equity: $28.5B + $0.1B (net cash) = $28.6B
Implied price: $28.6B / 117M = ~$244
vs current: -17%
```

**Base Case: +5%/year Y1-5, +3%/year Y6-10**

```
Year 5 FCF: $3.11B x (1.05)^5 = $3.11B x 1.276 = $3.97B
Year 10 FCF: $3.97B x (1.03)^5 = $3.97B x 1.159 = $4.60B
Terminal value: $4.60B / 0.07 = $65.7B
PV of terminal: $65.7B / 2.594 = $25.3B
PV of interim FCFs: ~$20.9B
```

**Bull Case: +10%/year Y1-5, +5%/year Y6-10**

#### Earnings Quality Assessment

The spread between gross margin and operating margin shows how much revenue is consumed by operating expenses beyond cost of goods sold. A narrowing spread means the company is leveraging its fixed costs.

```
Q3 2024: Gross Margin ~82%, Operating Margin ~18%, Spread = 64pp
Q2 2026: Gross Margin ~84%, Operating Margin ~26%, Spread = 58pp
Improvement: 6 percentage points of spread compression over ~2 years
```

Spread compression during peak quarters from 64pp to 58pp signals improving operating leverage -- fixed costs (technology, marketing infrastructure) spread over growing revenue.

**Test 3: EPS Trend**

```
2022 EPS: $2.17
2025 EPS: $9.81
3-Year CAGR: 65.2% (computed above)
Direction: strongly positive
```

**Scorecard summary:**

| Factor | Rating | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|

| SBC transparency | Poor | Data missing; estimated $0.7--1.5B |
| Working capital quality | Moderate | Merchant model inflates OCF; reversible |

#### Reverse DCF (Detailed)

The reverse DCF answers: "What FCF growth rate justifies the current stock price?"

**Setup:**

```
Known: EV = $34.3B, Current FCF = $3.11B, WACC = 10%, Terminal Growth = 3%
Unknown: Implied FCF growth rate (g) for years 1-10
```

**Perpetuity check (no growth period, just terminal value):**

```
If FCF stays at $3.11B forever:
  PV = $3.11B / (WACC - g) = $3.11B / (0.10 - 0.03) = $44.4B
  This exceeds current EV by $10.1B (29% premium)
```

**Conclusion**: the market is not even pricing in flat FCF. At a 10% WACC, the implied growth rate is NEGATIVE. Solving for the break-even WACC:

```
WACC_implied = (FCF / EV) + Terminal Growth
             = ($3.11B / $34.3B) + 0.03
             = 0.0907 + 0.03
             = 12.07%
```

The market demands a ~12% return from EXPE -- 200bps above a standard 10% WACC -- reflecting the perceived risk of a cyclical, #2-positioned OTA.

#### SBC-Adjusted FCF and Earnings

SBC (stock-based compensation) is a non-cash charge that does not reduce FCF but does dilute shareholders. The report estimates SBC at 5--10% of revenue because the actual figure is missing.

**Why FCF is unaffected:**

```
FCF = Operating Cash Flow - CapEx
SBC is a non-cash expense: it reduces GAAP net income but does NOT
reduce cash flow. FCF of $3.11B is already the "cash truth."

The real cost of SBC is dilution: more shares outstanding over time,
reducing each existing shareholder's ownership percentage.
```

**Key takeaway**: The reported P/E of 18.5x is based on GAAP earnings that include SBC as an expense only implicitly through diluted share count. If SBC were deducted from net income (treating it as a real economic cost), the adjusted P/E ranges from 62x to N/A. The FCF yield of 9.0% is the more reliable valuation anchor because it reflects actual cash generation regardless of SBC accounting treatment.

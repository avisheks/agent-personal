## 12. Things the Market May Be Missing

**Underestimated Opportunities:**

1. **Permian Basin consolidation premium.** [INFERENCE] OXY is now one of the top 3 Permian operators post-CrownRock. In a take-private or merger scenario, the consolidated Permian acreage commands a premium. Berkshire's 28% stake makes any hostile bid virtually impossible, but a friendly deal is not out of the question.

2. **Carbon capture regulatory tailwinds.** [INFERENCE] The IRA's 45Q tax credits ($85/ton for DAC) could make 1PointFive economically viable at scale. If carbon markets develop (EU ETS prices, California cap-and-trade), OXY's first-mover position in DAC becomes a genuine growth driver rather than a science project.

3. **OxyChem hidden value.** [INFERENCE] The chemicals segment (OxyChem) generated $1.5-2B in annual EBITDA historically, providing counter-cyclical diversification when oil prices are weak. The market values OXY purely as an E&P, potentially undervaluing the chemicals contribution.

**Underestimated Risks:**

4. **OPEC+ discipline breakdown.** [INFERENCE] Saudi Arabia and Russia have signaled willingness to increase production. A coordinated supply increase of 2-3M bbl/day could push oil below $60, devastating OXY's earnings and FCF.

5. **Stranded asset risk.** [INFERENCE] Long-duration oil reserves face terminal value risk as the energy transition accelerates. OXY's Permian reserves are lower-cost (survivable longer), but the 20-30 year tail of proved reserves may never be developed if EV adoption and renewables continue on current trajectories.

6. **Preferred stock overhang.** [INFERENCE] Berkshire holds $10B in OXY preferred stock paying 8% annually ($800M/year). This is a significant cash drain that diverts FCF from common shareholders. The preferreds are callable but OXY may lack the liquidity to redeem them while simultaneously paying down acquisition debt.

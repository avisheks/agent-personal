## Executive Summary

**Bottom Line: Expedia Group is a turnaround story maturing into a steady compounder, trading at a compelling 11.7x forward P/E with a ~9% FCF yield. The stock offers deep value characteristics for a business generating $3.1B in annual free cash flow, though elevated volatility (44.7%) and a severe historical max drawdown (86.7%) demand careful position sizing.** **INFERENCE**

Expedia Group (EXPE) is the world's second-largest online travel agency, operating Expedia.com, Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, Travelocity, and Hotwire. The company has undergone a significant transformation since its COVID-era trough, with TTM revenue recovering to $14.7B and EPS reaching $9.81 -- a dramatic recovery from the -$2.89 EPS posted in 2020. **FACT**

**Key investment thesis points:**

- **Deep value at 11.7x forward P/E:** The forward multiple implies ~58% earnings growth, suggesting the market expects a significant step-up in profitability. At 18.5x trailing P/E, EXPE trades at a discount to the S&P 500. **FACT**
- **Strong FCF generation:** $3.11B in FCF on $14.7B revenue (21.1% FCF margin) with a ~9.0% FCF yield on market cap -- nearly double the S&P 500 average. **FACT**
- **Revenue acceleration:** Q2 2026 showed 14.0% YoY revenue growth, a significant acceleration from the 7.6% TTM growth, suggesting operational momentum. **FACT**
- **Vrbo differentiation:** The alternative accommodation platform provides a unique competitive angle against both BKNG and ABNB. **INFERENCE**
- **Multi-brand portfolio:** A diversified brand strategy (Expedia, Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz) covers different customer segments and price points. **INFERENCE**

**Primary risks:** Very high annualized volatility (44.7%), an 86.7% max drawdown, competitive pressure from BKNG globally and ABNB in alternative accommodations, Google's travel ambitions, and structural #2 positioning in the OTA market. **INFERENCE**

**Verdict:** EXPE is a contrarian value play within the OTA space. The 11.7x forward P/E and ~9% FCF yield provide a meaningful margin of safety, and the Q2 2026 revenue acceleration to 14% suggests the turnaround is gaining momentum. Best suited for value-oriented investors comfortable with high volatility. **INFERENCE**

## Contrarian Analysis

### The Bull Case Everyone Knows

The consensus bull case for EXPE is: cheap valuation, travel recovery, platform unification driving margins, Vrbo as an Airbnb alternative, and FCF supporting buybacks. This is a straightforward "cheap turnaround" thesis that value investors find appealing. **INFERENCE**

### Counter-Evidence and Contrarian Concerns

**1. Permanently #2 in a Winner-Take-Most Industry** **INFERENCE**
- OTA economics favor scale: more listings attract more traffic, which funds more marketing. BKNG has 28M+ listings and 2x EXPE's revenue.
- Being #2 in an industry with strong network effects may mean permanently lower margins and slower growth.
- EXPE has been "the cheaper OTA" for years without ever closing the gap.

**2. The Platform Unification Has Been Promised for Years** **INFERENCE**
- EXPE has been working on technology consolidation since at least 2020 under former CEO Peter Kern.
- The benefits always seem to be "around the corner" -- investors have heard this story before.
- The $770M in CapEx (5.2% of revenue) vs BKNG's 1.2% reflects this ongoing investment, with uncertain payoff timing.

**3. Vrbo Is Losing the Vacation Rental War** **INFERENCE**
- Airbnb dominates the alternative accommodation narrative and has expanded into traditional hotels.
- Vrbo's "whole home only" model limits its addressable market vs Airbnb's broader listing base.
- Hotels.com and Expedia.com compete against BKNG's Booking.com, the global leader, on the hotel side.

**4. FCF Quality Deserves Scrutiny** **INFERENCE**
- FCF/NI ratio is ~240% ($3.11B FCF vs $1.29B net income), which is unusually high.
- While high FCF/NI can indicate high earnings quality, it can also reflect working capital timing benefits (merchant model prepayments) that are not permanently sustainable.
- The gap between OCF ($3.88B) and net income ($1.29B) is large and warrants investigation.

**5. Macro Sensitivity Is Extreme** **INFERENCE**
- In 2020, EXPE revenue fell from $2.75B to $920M quarterly (-66%) and FCF turned deeply negative (-$4.63B).
- The 86.7% max drawdown -- worse than BKNG's 80.4% -- demonstrates that EXPE gets hit harder in downturns.
- As the #2 OTA, EXPE may lose disproportionate share during industry contractions as travelers consolidate to the market leader.

### What Would Change the Thesis

| Signal | Implication | Action |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Revenue growth drops below 3% for 2+ quarters | Turnaround stalling | Reduce position |
| Vrbo bookings decline YoY | Losing the vacation rental competition | Reassess |
| BKNG acquires market share at EXPE expense | Structural competitive decline | Exit |
| Platform unification delivers visible margin expansion | Thesis confirmed | Add to position |
| Forward P/E re-rates above 16x | Market recognizing quality improvement | Hold, trim if >20x |

## Things the Market May Be Missing

### Positive Surprises (Upside Optionality)

**1. B2B (Expedia Partner Solutions) Is Underappreciated** **INFERENCE**
- Expedia's B2B segment supplies hotel inventory to airlines, banks, loyalty programs, and other travel companies.
- This segment has lower customer acquisition costs than the consumer business and provides sticky, recurring revenue.
- As more companies embed travel into their products, B2B could become a significant growth driver with superior economics.

**2. The Q2 2026 Acceleration May Be Structural** **INFERENCE**
- Q2 2026 revenue growth of 14.0% was nearly double the 7.6% TTM growth rate and the highest since the post-COVID recovery period.
- EPS of $7.16 in Q2 2026 alone (vs $2.48 in Q2 2025, a 189% increase) suggests a non-linear earnings trajectory.
- If this acceleration reflects platform unification benefits materializing, the forward earnings power is substantially higher than the trailing $9.81 EPS implies.

**3. The FCF Yield Gap vs BKNG Is Too Wide** **INFERENCE**
- EXPE yields ~9.0% on FCF vs BKNG's ~6.8%. Both are OTAs in the same industry.
- The ~220bps gap implies the market views EXPE's FCF as 30%+ riskier than BKNG's.
- While some discount is warranted for scale and competitive position, the current gap may be excessive.

**4. AI-Powered Personalization Could Narrow the Gap with BKNG** **INFERENCE**
- AI tools can improve search relevance, pricing optimization, and customer service -- areas where technology can offset scale disadvantages.
- EXPE's ongoing platform unification positions it to deploy AI more effectively across a unified data set.
- A smaller company can sometimes be more agile in adopting new technology paradigms.

**5. Share Buybacks at These Valuations Are Highly Accretive** **INFERENCE**
- Buying back stock at a 9% FCF yield (11x FCF) is extremely value-accretive.
- If EXPE deploys $2B+ annually in buybacks at current prices, it retires ~6% of shares per year.
- At these valuations, every dollar of buyback creates more per-share value than almost any alternative use of capital.

### Negative Surprises (Downside Risks Underpriced)

**1. The Forward P/E Gap May Signal a Data Anomaly** **INFERENCE**
- A 37% gap between trailing P/E (18.5x) and forward P/E (11.7x) is unusually large.
- If analyst estimates are too aggressive and earnings disappoint, the stock could reprice to trailing multiples, erasing the "cheap forward" thesis.

**2. Travel Is a Consumer Discretionary Exposed to Recession** **INFERENCE**
- Cumulative inflation since 2020 has strained consumer budgets. Travel is among the first discretionary cuts in a downturn.
- EXPE's 86.7% max drawdown demonstrates the stock's extreme vulnerability to macro shocks. **FACT**

**3. Google's Vertical Travel Integration** **INFERENCE**
- Google Flights, Hotels, and Travel increasingly serve as the starting point for travel research.
- EXPE's marketing spend is heavily weighted toward performance marketing (primarily Google), creating structural dependency on a competitor.
- A shift toward Google's own booking tools could disintermediate OTAs, with EXPE more vulnerable than BKNG given its smaller scale.

### 1. Valuation Metrics

**P/E (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)**
What the market pays per dollar of earnings. Higher = more expensive relative to current profits.
> P/E = Stock Price ÷ EPS = $100 ÷ $5.00 = **20.0x**
> Interpretation: investors pay $20 for every $1 of Acme's annual profit.

**Forward P/E**
P/E using *next year's estimated* earnings instead of trailing. Lower than trailing P/E when earnings are expected to grow.
> If analysts estimate next year EPS = $6.50:
> Forward P/E = $100 ÷ $6.50 = **15.4x**

**PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings-to-Growth)**
P/E divided by expected EPS growth rate. Adjusts valuation for growth speed. PEG < 1 may suggest undervaluation.
> If EPS growth rate = 25%:
> PEG = 20.0 ÷ 25 = **0.80**
> Interpretation: paying less than 1x the growth rate — potentially attractive.

**EV (Enterprise Value)**
Total company value including debt, minus cash. What an acquirer would pay to buy the whole business.
> EV = Market Cap + Total Debt − Cash
> EV = ($100 × 10M) + $200M − $50M = $1,000M + $200M − $50M = **$1,150M**

**EV/EBITDA**
Enterprise value per dollar of operating cash earnings. Lower = cheaper. Useful for comparing companies with different debt levels.
> EV/EBITDA = $1,150M ÷ $90M = **12.8x**
> Interpretation: the business is valued at 12.8 years of its operating cash earnings.

**EV/Sales (EV/Revenue)**
Enterprise value per dollar of revenue. Used for high-growth companies that may not yet be profitable.
> EV/Sales = $1,150M ÷ $500M = **2.3x**

**P/S (Price-to-Sales)**
Market cap per dollar of revenue. Simpler than EV/Sales (ignores debt/cash).
> P/S = Market Cap ÷ Revenue = $1,000M ÷ $500M = **2.0x**

**P/B (Price-to-Book)**
Market price relative to accounting book value. P/B < 1 means market values the company below its asset base.
> P/B = Stock Price ÷ Book Value per Share = $100 ÷ $30 = **3.3x**

**FCF Yield (Free Cash Flow Yield)**
Free cash flow as a percentage of market cap. Higher = more cash generation per dollar of market value. Inverse of P/FCF.
> FCF Yield = FCF ÷ Market Cap = $40M ÷ $1,000M = **4.0%**
> Interpretation: Acme generates 4 cents of free cash for every $1 of market value annually.

**Dividend Yield**
Annual dividends as a percentage of stock price.
> Dividend Yield = Dividends/Share ÷ Price = $1.50 ÷ $100 = **1.5%**

---

### 2. Profitability Metrics

**Gross Margin**
Revenue remaining after direct production costs. Measures pricing power and production efficiency.
> Gross Margin = Gross Profit ÷ Revenue = $200M ÷ $500M = **40.0%**
> Interpretation: Acme keeps 40 cents of every revenue dollar after paying for what it sells.

**Operating Margin**
Revenue remaining after all operating expenses (but before interest and taxes). Core business profitability.
> Operating Margin = Operating Income ÷ Revenue = $75M ÷ $500M = **15.0%**

**Net Margin**
Bottom-line profit per dollar of revenue, after all costs including taxes and interest.
> Net Margin = Net Income ÷ Revenue = $50M ÷ $500M = **10.0%**

**ROE (Return on Equity)**
Profit generated per dollar of shareholder equity. Measures how efficiently the company uses shareholder capital.
> ROE = Net Income ÷ Total Equity = $50M ÷ $300M = **16.7%**
> Interpretation: for every $1 of equity, Acme generates 16.7 cents of annual profit.

**ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)**
Profit generated per dollar of ALL capital deployed (equity + debt). Better than ROE for comparing companies with different leverage.
> NOPAT = Operating Income × (1 − Tax Rate) = $75M × 0.75 = $56.25M
> ROIC = NOPAT ÷ Invested Capital = $56.25M ÷ $450M = **12.5%**
> Interpretation: Acme earns 12.5% on every dollar of capital invested. Compare to WACC (10%) — if ROIC > WACC, the company creates value.

---

### 3. Cash Flow Metrics

**OCF (Operating Cash Flow)**
Cash generated from normal business operations. Starts from net income, adds back non-cash charges, adjusts for working capital changes.
> OCF = $70M (from cash flow statement)

**FCF (Free Cash Flow)**
Cash available after maintaining/expanding the asset base. The cash that could be returned to shareholders or used for growth.
> FCF = Operating Cash Flow − CapEx = $70M − $30M = **$40M**

**CapEx (Capital Expenditures)**
Spending on physical assets (factories, equipment, data centers). A measure of investment intensity.
> CapEx = $30M
> CapEx/Revenue = $30M ÷ $500M = 6.0% (capital-light if <10%, capital-heavy if >20%)

**FCF/NI (FCF-to-Net-Income Ratio)**
Cash quality indicator. FCF/NI > 100% means the company generates more cash than reported profit — high earnings quality. FCF/NI < 80% may signal aggressive accounting.
> FCF/NI = $40M ÷ $50M = **80%**

---

### 4. Growth Metrics

**TTM (Trailing Twelve Months)**
Sum of the last four quarters. Used to annualize quarterly data without calendar-year bias.
> TTM Revenue = Q3 + Q4 + Q1 + Q2 = $120M + $130M + $125M + $125M = **$500M**

**YoY (Year-over-Year)**
Growth rate comparing the same period to the prior year. Removes seasonality.
> If Q2 2026 revenue = $125M and Q2 2025 revenue = $100M:
> YoY Growth = ($125M − $100M) ÷ $100M = **25.0%**

**CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)**
Smoothed annualized growth rate over multiple years. Accounts for compounding.
> If revenue was $200M five years ago and is $500M now:
> CAGR = ($500M/$200M)^(1/5) − 1 = (2.5)^0.2 − 1 = **20.1%**
> Interpretation: revenue grew at an average of 20.1% per year, compounded.

**EPS (Earnings Per Share)**
Net income allocated to each share. The denominator for P/E.
> EPS = Net Income ÷ Shares Outstanding = $50M ÷ 10M = **$5.00**

---

### 5. Risk Metrics

**Annualized Volatility**
Standard deviation of daily returns, annualized. Higher = more price uncertainty. Typical ranges: <15% low, 15-25% medium, 25-40% high, >40% very high.
> If daily return std dev = 2.0%:
> Annualized Vol = 2.0% × √252 = 2.0% × 15.87 = **31.7%** (high)

**Max Drawdown**
Largest peak-to-trough decline in stock price. Measures worst-case historical loss.
> If stock peaked at $150 and bottomed at $60:
> Max Drawdown = ($60 − $150) ÷ $150 = **−60%**

**VaR 95% (Value at Risk, 95th percentile)**
The daily loss that is exceeded only 5% of trading days. A "bad day" threshold.
> If 5th percentile of daily returns = −3.2%:
> VaR 95% = **−3.2%** (on 95% of days, you lose less than 3.2%)

**Beta**
Stock's sensitivity to market movements. Beta 1.0 = moves with market. Beta 1.5 = 50% more volatile than market. Beta 0.7 = 30% less volatile.
> If Acme's beta = 1.3:
> When S&P 500 falls 10%, Acme is expected to fall ~13%.

**Debt/Equity**
Total debt relative to shareholder equity. Measures financial leverage.
> D/E = Total Debt ÷ Total Equity = $200M ÷ $300M = **0.67**
> Interpretation: for every $1 of equity, Acme has $0.67 of debt. Under 1.0 is generally conservative.

### 6. Valuation Models (used in this report)

**Owner-Earnings Value (Buffett method)**
Intrinsic value based on sustainable cash earnings. Conservative — applies a 25% margin of safety haircut.
> Owner Earnings = Net Income + Depreciation − Maintenance CapEx
> Value = Owner Earnings × Growth Multiplier × (1 − 25% MoS)

**DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)**
Projects future free cash flows and discounts them to present value. The "gold standard" of intrinsic value — but only as good as the assumptions.
> Value = Σ(FCF_t ÷ (1 + WACC)^t) + Terminal Value
> Run three scenarios: Bear (low growth), Base (consensus), Bull (high growth)

**Graham Number**
Benjamin Graham's conservative intrinsic value formula. Suitable for stable, profitable companies.
> Graham Number = √(22.5 × EPS × Book Value per Share)
> = √(22.5 × $5.00 × $30) = √$3,375 = **$58.09**
> Interpretation: Graham would pay no more than $58.09 for Acme.

**EV/EBITDA Relative Value**
Per-share value implied by applying the sector median EV/EBITDA multiple.
> If sector median EV/EBITDA = 10x:
> Implied EV = 10 × $90M = $900M
> Implied Equity = $900M − $200M + $50M = $750M
> Implied Price = $750M ÷ 10M shares = **$75.00**

**PEG-Based Fair Value**
Fair P/E = EPS growth rate (Peter Lynch rule of thumb). PEG = 1 means "fair."
> If growth = 25%, fair P/E = 25x
> Fair Price = 25 × $5.00 = **$125.00**

## 12. Things Market May Be Missing

### 1. FCF Margin Trajectory Is Structural, Not Cyclical

The market may be treating EXPE's FCF margin expansion (14.1% in FY2023 to 21.1% in FY2025) as cyclical (peak travel demand) rather than structural (platform unification). If it is structural, then even in a travel slowdown, FCF margins would compress less than historically, providing downside protection. [INFERENCE]

### 2. The Vrbo Opportunity Is Larger Than Appreciated

Vrbo was struggling pre-COVID and during the initial recovery. Under the new leadership, it has been integrated into the One Key loyalty program, giving it access to Expedia's 100M+ member base. If Vrbo can capture even modest share from Airbnb in the vacation rental segment, it represents billions in incremental revenue at high margins. [INFERENCE]

### 3. B2B Revenue Creates Platform Value

Expedia Partner Solutions (EPS) is effectively a travel-as-a-service platform. As more companies embed travel into their products (fintech apps, airline ancillary revenue, bank loyalty programs), EPS is positioned to capture this demand without the customer acquisition costs of B2C. The market may be valuing EXPE purely as a B2C OTA and missing the platform economics. [INFERENCE]

### 4. Buyback Math Is Powerful at These Valuations

At ~9.3% FCF yield, if EXPE devotes 70% of FCF to buybacks (~$2.2B/year), it would retire roughly 6-7% of shares annually at current prices. Over three years, that compounds to 18-20% fewer shares, adding ~$2/year to EPS purely from financial engineering. Combined with organic growth, this could drive EPS to $18-20 by FY2028 without margin expansion. [INFERENCE/FORECAST]

### 5. The Forward P/E Disconnect

The gap between trailing P/E (17.57x) and forward P/E (11.42x) implies the market expects massive earnings growth ahead but is not fully pricing it in. This disconnect often resolves to the upside when earnings delivery confirms expectations. [INFERENCE]

### 1. Valuation -- Worked Examples

#### P/E (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)

What the market pays per dollar of current earnings. Higher means more expensive relative to today's profits.

**Formula:** P/E = Stock Price / Trailing EPS

**EXPE:**
```
P/E = $279.35 / $9.81 = 28.48x
```
Report states trailing P/E of 17.57x, which reflects a different EPS base (likely TTM including more recent quarters with higher earnings, or adjusted EPS). The discrepancy highlights that P/E depends on which earnings figure is used -- GAAP trailing, adjusted trailing, or TTM.

**Interpretation:** At 17.57x trailing, investors pay $17.57 for every $1 of EXPE's annual profit. This is below the S&P 500 average of ~22x and well below peers BKNG (~28x) and ABNB (~35x).

---

#### Forward P/E

P/E using next year's estimated earnings instead of trailing. Lower than trailing when earnings are expected to grow.

**Formula:** Forward P/E = Stock Price / Forward EPS Estimate

**EXPE:**
```
Forward P/E = 11.42x (reported)
Implied Forward EPS = $279.35 / 11.42 = $24.46
```
The large gap between trailing P/E (17.57x) and forward P/E (11.42x) implies consensus expects ~54% EPS growth. This could reflect FY2027 estimates if analysts project continued acceleration from the Q2 FY2026 growth rate.

**Interpretation:** At 11.42x forward, EXPE is among the cheapest large-cap internet stocks. Either estimates are too optimistic (risk), or the market is not fully pricing in the growth (opportunity).

---

#### EV (Enterprise Value)

Total company value including debt, minus cash. What an acquirer would pay to own the whole business.

**Formula:** EV = Market Cap + Total Debt - Cash

**EXPE:**
```
Market Cap = $33.5B
EV = $33.4B
EV - Market Cap = -$0.1B
```
The fact that EV is roughly equal to (slightly below) Market Cap means EXPE has near-zero net debt or a slight net cash position. This is notable because EXPE historically carried heavy debt from COVID-era borrowing -- the balance sheet has improved materially.

Enterprise value per dollar of operating cash earnings. Useful for comparing companies with different debt levels.

**Formula:** EV/EBITDA = Enterprise Value / EBITDA

**EXPE:**

**Peer comparison:**

EXPE trades at a 28% discount to BKNG and 42% discount to ABNB on this metric, reflecting the market's lingering skepticism about EXPE's competitive position.

### 2. Profitability -- Worked Examples

#### Net Margin

Bottom-line profit per dollar of revenue, after all costs including taxes and interest.

**Formula:** Net Margin = Net Income / Revenue

**EXPE:**
```

```
Not elite, but the more meaningful profitability story is FCF margin (see below).

#### FCF Margin

Free cash flow per dollar of revenue. For asset-light businesses like OTAs, FCF margin is often more informative than net margin because non-cash charges (D&A, stock comp) create a gap between GAAP profit and cash generation.

**Formula:** FCF Margin = Free Cash Flow / Revenue

**EXPE trajectory:**
```
FY2023: $1.8B / $12.8B = 14.1%
FY2024: $2.3B / $13.7B = 16.8%
FY2025: $3.1B / $14.73B = 21.1%
```
FCF margins expanded 7 percentage points in two years while revenue grew 15%. This operating leverage is the core investment thesis -- the platform unification means each incremental dollar of revenue drops more cash to the bottom line.

---

#### Operating Leverage

When profits grow faster than revenue, the business has operating leverage. This happens when fixed costs are spread over a larger revenue base.

**EXPE (FY2023 to FY2025):**
```
Revenue growth:  $14.73B / $12.8B - 1 = +15.1%
FCF growth:      $3.11B / $1.8B - 1   = +72.8%
Leverage ratio:  72.8% / 15.1%        = 4.8x
```
For every 1% of revenue growth, FCF grew 4.8%. This extraordinary leverage reflects the one-time structural benefit of consolidating duplicate technology platforms and brands onto a single stack.

**Interpretation:** Earnings quality is high. The business generates far more real cash than GAAP accounting reports as profit. This is the opposite of a red flag -- companies with FCF/NI below 80% deserve scrutiny for aggressive accounting.

### 5. Risk -- Worked Examples

#### Annualized Volatility

Standard deviation of daily returns, scaled to a full year. Higher means wider price swings.

**Formula:** Annual Vol = Daily Std Dev x sqrt(252)

**EXPE:**
```
Annualized Volatility = 44.7%
Implied Daily Std Dev = 44.7% / sqrt(252) = 44.7% / 15.87 = ~2.82%
```
**Rating scale:** <15% low, 15-25% medium, 25-40% high, >40% very high.

At 44.7%, EXPE is "very high" volatility. On a typical day, the stock moves roughly 2.8% in either direction. In a month (~21 trading days), a one-standard-deviation move is 44.7% x sqrt(21/252) = ~12.9%.

#### Max Drawdown

Largest peak-to-trough decline in stock price history.

**Formula:** Max Drawdown = (Trough - Peak) / Peak

**EXPE:**
```
Max Drawdown = 86.7% (COVID era)
```
If the stock peaked at $X, it fell to $X x (1 - 0.867) = 0.133 x $X. This is an extreme historical drawdown driven by the pandemic collapse in travel. While unlikely to repeat at that magnitude, it illustrates the cyclical risk in travel stocks.

The daily loss exceeded only 5% of trading days. A "bad day" benchmark.

On 95% of trading days, EXPE loses less than 3.8%. On a $10,000 position, this means 19 out of 20 days, losses stay below $380. On the worst 1-in-20 day, losses exceed $380.

**Dollar impact at position sizes:**

### D.11 Glossary of Terms Used

All terms below are referenced in the EXPE report. Definitions include worked examples using a fictional "Acme Corp" (Stock Price $100, Shares 10M, Revenue $500M, Net Income $50M, EBITDA $90M, FCF $40M, Debt $200M, Cash $50M, Equity $300M, EPS $5.00).

**P/E (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)** -- What the market pays per dollar of earnings. Higher = more expensive relative to current profits.
> P/E = Price / EPS = $100 / $5.00 = 20.0x. Investors pay $20 for every $1 of annual profit.

**Forward P/E** -- P/E using next year's estimated earnings. Lower than trailing P/E when earnings growth is expected.
> If estimated EPS = $6.50: Forward P/E = $100 / $6.50 = 15.4x.

**EV (Enterprise Value)** -- Total company value including debt minus cash. What an acquirer would pay for the whole business.
> EV = Market Cap + Debt - Cash = $1,000M + $200M - $50M = $1,150M.

**EV/EBITDA** -- Enterprise value per dollar of operating cash earnings. Lower = cheaper. Useful for cross-company comparison regardless of capital structure.
> EV/EBITDA = $1,150M / $90M = 12.8x.

**EV/Sales** -- Enterprise value per dollar of revenue.
> EV/Sales = $1,150M / $500M = 2.3x.

**P/S (Price-to-Sales)** -- Market cap per dollar of revenue.
> P/S = $1,000M / $500M = 2.0x.

**FCF Yield (Free Cash Flow Yield)** -- Free cash flow as a percentage of market cap. Higher = more cash generation per dollar of market value.
> FCF Yield = $40M / $1,000M = 4.0%. Acme generates 4 cents of free cash for every $1 of market value.

**OCF (Operating Cash Flow)** -- Cash generated from normal business operations. Starts from net income, adds back non-cash charges, adjusts for working capital.

**FCF (Free Cash Flow)** -- Cash available after maintaining/expanding the asset base. Can be returned to shareholders or used for growth.
> FCF = OCF - CapEx = $70M - $30M = $40M.

**CapEx (Capital Expenditures)** -- Spending on physical assets. Capital-light if <10% of revenue, capital-heavy if >20%.
> CapEx/Revenue = $30M / $500M = 6.0%.

**FCF/NI (FCF-to-Net-Income Ratio)** -- Cash quality indicator. FCF/NI > 100% = company generates more cash than reported profit (high quality). FCF/NI < 80% may signal aggressive accounting.
> FCF/NI = $40M / $50M = 80%.

**EPS (Earnings Per Share)** -- Net income allocated to each share.
> EPS = $50M / 10M = $5.00.

**YoY (Year-over-Year)** -- Growth rate comparing the same period to the prior year. Removes seasonality.

**CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)** -- Smoothed annualized growth rate over multiple years.
> If revenue grew from $200M to $500M in 5 years: CAGR = (2.5)^(1/5) - 1 = 20.1%.

**SMA (Simple Moving Average)** -- Average closing price over a specified number of days. The 50-day SMA tracks short-term trend; the 200-day SMA tracks long-term trend. A "golden cross" occurs when the 50-day crosses above the 200-day, signaling bullish momentum.

**Annualized Volatility** -- Standard deviation of daily returns, annualized. Ranges: <15% low, 15-25% medium, 25-40% high, >40% very high.
> Daily std dev of 2.0% x sqrt(252) = 31.7%.

**Max Drawdown** -- Largest peak-to-trough decline in stock price. Measures worst-case historical loss.
> Peak $150, trough $60: Max Drawdown = -60%.

**VaR 95% (Value at Risk)** -- The daily loss exceeded only 5% of trading days. A "bad day" threshold.

**Beta** -- Stock's sensitivity to market movements. Beta 1.0 = moves with market. Beta 1.5 = 50% more volatile.
> Beta 1.3: when S&P 500 falls 10%, stock expected to fall ~13%.

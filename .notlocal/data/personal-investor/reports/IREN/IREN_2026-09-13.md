## 2. Company Overview

**Iris Energy (IREN)** is an Australian-founded, NASDAQ-listed company operating at the intersection of two of the most capital-intensive industries in technology: Bitcoin mining and AI/HPC data center infrastructure.

### Business Model

IREN operates large-scale data center facilities powered primarily by renewable energy sources. The company's business has two principal segments:

1. **Bitcoin Mining:** IREN deploys proprietary and hosted mining hardware (ASICs) to mine Bitcoin. Revenue is directly tied to Bitcoin price, network hash rate, and mining difficulty. The April 2024 Bitcoin halving reduced block rewards from 6.25 BTC to 3.125 BTC, cutting mining revenue per unit of hash power in half.

2. **AI/HPC Data Centers:** IREN is actively pivoting its infrastructure toward AI and high-performance computing (HPC) workloads. The company leverages its existing land, power agreements, and cooling infrastructure to build GPU-dense data center capacity for AI training and inference workloads. This pivot is strategic: AI compute commands significantly higher margins per megawatt than Bitcoin mining.

### Key Assets

- **Power Infrastructure:** IREN has secured access to significant power capacity across multiple sites, a critical competitive moat in the data center industry where power is the binding constraint.
- **Land Holdings:** Large-scale sites suitable for data center expansion, particularly in regions with favorable energy costs and regulatory environments.
- **Cooling & Facility Infrastructure:** Existing cooling systems and facility designs that can be repurposed from mining to AI/HPC workloads.

### Strategic Positioning

IREN sits at a unique crossroads. The AI data center buildout is one of the largest infrastructure investment cycles in history, with hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) committing hundreds of billions in capital expenditure. Companies that can deliver power-ready, GPU-dense data center capacity are in high demand. IREN's thesis is that its existing renewable power agreements, land, and operational expertise in running high-density compute facilities give it a head start in capturing this demand.

However, the transition from Bitcoin mining to AI/HPC is non-trivial. Bitcoin mining facilities are architecturally simpler (uniform ASIC hardware, lower networking requirements) compared to AI training clusters (heterogeneous GPU hardware, high-bandwidth networking, complex cooling, strict uptime SLAs). IREN must demonstrate it can execute this transition at scale while managing the inherent volatility of its legacy Bitcoin mining business.

This is a significant negative signal. Revenue is growing, but costs and/or non-cash charges are growing even faster.

**Net Margin Estimate (Q2 2026):** -$702.6M / $707.0M = **-99.4%**. The company essentially lost a dollar for every dollar of revenue in its most recent quarter.

### Profitability Verdict

IREN is not a profitable company by any consistent measure. The two quarters of profitability (Q2-Q3 2025) appear to have been driven by favorable conditions (likely Bitcoin price and/or one-time gains) rather than structural profitability. ### Risk Factor Analysis

**1. Bitcoin Price Risk (Critical)**
IREN's legacy mining business is directly tied to Bitcoin price. The April 2024 halving cut block rewards in half, and any significant BTC price decline would simultaneously reduce mining revenue, trigger asset impairments, and potentially impair the company's ability to fund its pivot to AI/HPC.

**2. Execution Risk (Critical)**
The pivot from Bitcoin mining to AI/HPC data centers is IREN's core strategic thesis, but it is unproven at scale. Building enterprise-grade AI training clusters requires capabilities beyond what Bitcoin mining demands: high-bandwidth networking, redundant power systems, strict uptime SLAs, sophisticated cooling for GPU-dense racks, and enterprise customer management.

**3. Capital Market Risk (High)**
IREN burns billions in free cash flow and must continuously raise capital. If equity markets deteriorate (making stock issuance dilutive at low prices) or credit markets tighten (raising debt costs), IREN could face a liquidity crisis that forces it to curtail growth plans.

**4. Competition Risk (High)**
The AI data center buildout has attracted enormous capital and competition. Established players (Equinix, Digital Realty, QTS), hyperscaler self-builds (Microsoft, Google, Amazon), and other Bitcoin-mining-to-AI pivots (Core Scientific, Hut 8) are all competing for the same customers. IREN's competitive advantages (power, land) are real but not unique.

**5. Technology Risk (Moderate)**
AI hardware evolves rapidly. Today's GPU architecture (NVIDIA H100/B200) may be superseded by next-generation hardware that requires different facility specifications. IREN must build facilities that are flexible enough to accommodate hardware evolution.

**6. Regulatory Risk (Moderate)**
Data center power consumption is increasingly scrutinized by regulators. Permitting delays, environmental regulations, or electricity pricing changes could impact IREN's buildout timeline and economics.

**7. Dilution Risk (High)**
Continuous capital raises dilute existing shareholders. The share count has likely grown significantly over the analysis period, which partially explains why a company with $707M in quarterly revenue and a ~$16.8B market cap has such a low share price ($24.45).

### The 98.6% Max Drawdown

This metric deserves special emphasis. A 98.6% drawdown means that an investor who bought at the peak would have lost 98.6 cents of every dollar invested. While the stock has recovered from that trough (it is currently at $24.45), this drawdown history demonstrates the extreme downside risk inherent in IREN shares. Very few stocks recover from drawdowns of this magnitude, and those that do often reward only those who bought at or near the bottom.

### Risk Verdict

IREN is the highest-risk stock in this analysis batch by every quantitative measure. The 115.3% annualized volatility means the stock's standard deviation of returns is larger than the returns themselves. The 98.6% max drawdown is near the theoretical maximum. The VaR metric implies a 1-in-20 chance of losing nearly 12% in a single day. This is a stock suitable only for investors with extremely high risk tolerance and a speculative allocation framework.

**5. Bitcoin Optionality**
While the AI/HPC pivot is the primary thesis, IREN retains significant exposure to Bitcoin. If BTC appreciates meaningfully (which is plausible given historical halving cycles and institutional adoption), IREN's mining operations could generate substantial additional revenue and profit, providing a free call option on Bitcoin price appreciation.

**6. Strategic Value**
IREN's power-connected, data-center-ready sites have significant strategic value. In an extreme bull case, IREN could be acquired by a hyperscaler, private equity firm, or infrastructure fund at a premium to its current market value.

## 13. Bear Case

### Thesis: Capital-Intensive Pivot with No Margin of Safety

**1. Profitability Is a Mirage**
IREN has produced only two profitable quarters in its entire history, both of which appear to have been driven by favorable one-time conditions. The most recent quarter (Q2 2026) showed a $702.6 million loss on $707 million in revenue — a net margin of approximately -99.4%. The company is not structurally profitable and may not become so for years.

**2. Cash Burn Is Unsustainable**
Cumulative FCF burn of approximately -$2.78 billion over just three quarters (Q4 2025 through Q2 2026) is extraordinary. IREN must continuously raise capital to fund this burn rate. Each equity raise dilutes existing shareholders; each debt issuance increases financial leverage and interest expense. If capital markets become less accommodating — due to recession, rising rates, or sector rotation — IREN could face a funding crisis.

**3. The 98.6% Drawdown Can Happen Again**
IREN has already demonstrated that it can lose nearly all its value. The underlying risk factors have not fundamentally changed: the company is still unprofitable, still capital-intensive, still exposed to Bitcoin price volatility, and still dependent on continuous capital market access. The previous near-total loss is not a historical curiosity — it is a warning.

**4. Bitcoin Halving Squeeze**
The April 2024 halving cut Bitcoin mining rewards in half. Unless Bitcoin price doubles to compensate, mining profitability is permanently impaired. If IREN is still deriving significant revenue from mining, this is a structural headwind that cannot be managed away.

**5. AI Buildout Overcapacity Risk**
The current AI data center buildout is driven by massive capital commitments from a small number of hyperscalers. If AI demand disappoints (slower enterprise adoption, model efficiency improvements reducing compute needs, regulatory constraints), the data center industry could face overcapacity, compressing prices and margins for all providers, including IREN.

**6. Valuation Leaves No Room for Error**
At EV/Sales of 25.9x and EV/EBITDA of 478x, IREN is priced for perfection and beyond. ### 1. Valuation Metrics

**P/E (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)**
What the market pays per dollar of earnings. Higher = more expensive relative to current profits.
> P/E = Stock Price / EPS = $100 / $5.00 = **20.0x**
> Interpretation: investors pay $20 for every $1 of Acme's annual profit.

**Forward P/E**
P/E using *next year's estimated* earnings instead of trailing. Lower than trailing P/E when earnings are expected to grow.
> If analysts estimate next year EPS = $6.50:
> Forward P/E = $100 / $6.50 = **15.4x**

**PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings-to-Growth)**
P/E divided by expected EPS growth rate. Adjusts valuation for growth speed. PEG < 1 may suggest undervaluation.
> If EPS growth rate = 25%:
> PEG = 20.0 / 25 = **0.80**
> Interpretation: paying less than 1x the growth rate — potentially attractive.

**EV (Enterprise Value)**
Total company value including debt, minus cash. What an acquirer would pay to buy the whole business.
> EV = Market Cap + Total Debt - Cash
> EV = ($100 x 10M) + $200M - $50M = $1,000M + $200M - $50M = **$1,150M**

**EV/EBITDA**
Enterprise value per dollar of operating cash earnings. Lower = cheaper. Useful for comparing companies with different debt levels.
> EV/EBITDA = $1,150M / $90M = **12.8x**
> Interpretation: the business is valued at 12.8 years of its operating cash earnings.

**EV/Sales (EV/Revenue)**
Enterprise value per dollar of revenue. Used for high-growth companies that may not yet be profitable.
> EV/Sales = $1,150M / $500M = **2.3x**

**P/S (Price-to-Sales)**
Market cap per dollar of revenue. Simpler than EV/Sales (ignores debt/cash).
> P/S = Market Cap / Revenue = $1,000M / $500M = **2.0x**

**P/B (Price-to-Book)**
Market price relative to accounting book value. P/B < 1 means market values the company below its asset base.
> P/B = Stock Price / Book Value per Share = $100 / $30 = **3.3x**

**FCF Yield (Free Cash Flow Yield)**
Free cash flow as a percentage of market cap. Higher = more cash generation per dollar of market value. Inverse of P/FCF.
> FCF Yield = FCF / Market Cap = $40M / $1,000M = **4.0%**
> Interpretation: Acme generates 4 cents of free cash for every $1 of market value annually.

**Dividend Yield**
Annual dividends as a percentage of stock price.
> Dividend Yield = Dividends/Share / Price = $1.50 / $100 = **1.5%**

---

### 2. Profitability Metrics

**Gross Margin**
Revenue remaining after direct production costs. Measures pricing power and production efficiency.
> Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue = $200M / $500M = **40.0%**
> Interpretation: Acme keeps 40 cents of every revenue dollar after paying for what it sells.

**Operating Margin**
Revenue remaining after all operating expenses (but before interest and taxes). Core business profitability.
> Operating Margin = Operating Income / Revenue = $75M / $500M = **15.0%**

**Net Margin**
Bottom-line profit per dollar of revenue, after all costs including taxes and interest.
> Net Margin = Net Income / Revenue = $50M / $500M = **10.0%**

**ROE (Return on Equity)**
Profit generated per dollar of shareholder equity. Measures how efficiently the company uses shareholder capital.
> ROE = Net Income / Total Equity = $50M / $300M = **16.7%**
> Interpretation: for every $1 of equity, Acme generates 16.7 cents of annual profit.

**ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)**
Profit generated per dollar of ALL capital deployed (equity + debt). Better than ROE for comparing companies with different leverage.
> NOPAT = Operating Income x (1 - Tax Rate) = $75M x 0.75 = $56.25M
> ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital = $56.25M / $450M = **12.5%**
> Interpretation: Acme earns 12.5% on every dollar of capital invested. Compare to WACC (10%) — if ROIC > WACC, the company creates value.

---

### 3. Cash Flow Metrics

**OCF (Operating Cash Flow)**
Cash generated from normal business operations. Starts from net income, adds back non-cash charges, adjusts for working capital changes.
> OCF = $70M (from cash flow statement)

**FCF (Free Cash Flow)**
Cash available after maintaining/expanding the asset base. The cash that could be returned to shareholders or used for growth.
> FCF = Operating Cash Flow - CapEx = $70M - $30M = **$40M**

**CapEx (Capital Expenditures)**
Spending on physical assets (factories, equipment, data centers). A measure of investment intensity.
> CapEx = $30M
> CapEx/Revenue = $30M / $500M = 6.0% (capital-light if <10%, capital-heavy if >20%)

**FCF/NI (FCF-to-Net-Income Ratio)**
Cash quality indicator. FCF/NI > 100% means the company generates more cash than reported profit — high earnings quality. FCF/NI < 80% may signal aggressive accounting.
> FCF/NI = $40M / $50M = **80%**

---

### 4. Growth Metrics

**TTM (Trailing Twelve Months)**
Sum of the last four quarters. Used to annualize quarterly data without calendar-year bias.
> TTM Revenue = Q3 + Q4 + Q1 + Q2 = $120M + $130M + $125M + $125M = **$500M**

**YoY (Year-over-Year)**
Growth rate comparing the same period to the prior year. Removes seasonality.
> If Q2 2026 revenue = $125M and Q2 2025 revenue = $100M:
> YoY Growth = ($125M - $100M) / $100M = **25.0%**

**CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)**
Smoothed annualized growth rate over multiple years. Accounts for compounding.
> If revenue was $200M five years ago and is $500M now:
> CAGR = ($500M/$200M)^(1/5) - 1 = (2.5)^0.2 - 1 = **20.1%**
> Interpretation: revenue grew at an average of 20.1% per year, compounded.

**EPS (Earnings Per Share)**
Net income allocated to each share. The denominator for P/E.
> EPS = Net Income / Shares Outstanding = $50M / 10M = **$5.00**

---

### 5. Risk Metrics

**Annualized Volatility**
Standard deviation of daily returns, annualized. Higher = more price uncertainty. Typical ranges: <15% low, 15-25% medium, 25-40% high, >40% very high.
> If daily return std dev = 2.0%:
> Annualized Vol = 2.0% x sqrt(252) = 2.0% x 15.87 = **31.7%** (high)

**Max Drawdown**
Largest peak-to-trough decline in stock price. Measures worst-case historical loss.
> If stock peaked at $150 and bottomed at $60:
> Max Drawdown = ($60 - $150) / $150 = **-60%**

**VaR 95% (Value at Risk, 95th percentile)**
The daily loss that is exceeded only 5% of trading days. A "bad day" threshold.
> If 5th percentile of daily returns = -3.2%:
> VaR 95% = **-3.2%** (on 95% of days, you lose less than 3.2%)

**Beta**
Stock's sensitivity to market movements. Beta 1.0 = moves with market. Beta 1.5 = 50% more volatile than market. Beta 0.7 = 30% less volatile.
> If Acme's beta = 1.3:
> When S&P 500 falls 10%, Acme is expected to fall ~13%.

**Debt/Equity**
Total debt relative to shareholder equity. Measures financial leverage.
> D/E = Total Debt / Total Equity = $200M / $300M = **0.67**
> Interpretation: for every $1 of equity, Acme has $0.67 of debt. Under 1.0 is generally conservative.

### 6. Valuation Models (used in this report)

**Owner-Earnings Value (Buffett method)**
Intrinsic value based on sustainable cash earnings. Conservative — applies a 25% margin of safety haircut.
> Owner Earnings = Net Income + Depreciation - Maintenance CapEx
> Value = Owner Earnings x Growth Multiplier x (1 - 25% MoS)

**DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)**
Projects future free cash flows and discounts them to present value. The "gold standard" of intrinsic value — but only as good as the assumptions.
> Value = Sum(FCF_t / (1 + WACC)^t) + Terminal Value
> Run three scenarios: Bear (low growth), Base (consensus), Bull (high growth)

**Graham Number**
Benjamin Graham's conservative intrinsic value formula. Suitable for stable, profitable companies.
> Graham Number = sqrt(22.5 x EPS x Book Value per Share)
> = sqrt(22.5 x $5.00 x $30) = sqrt($3,375) = **$58.09**
> Interpretation: Graham would pay no more than $58.09 for Acme.

**EV/EBITDA Relative Value**
Per-share value implied by applying the sector median EV/EBITDA multiple.
> If sector median EV/EBITDA = 10x:
> Implied EV = 10 x $90M = $900M
> Implied Equity = $900M - $200M + $50M = $750M
> Implied Price = $750M / 10M shares = **$75.00**

**PEG-Based Fair Value**
Fair P/E = EPS growth rate (Peter Lynch rule of thumb). PEG = 1 means "fair."
> If growth = 25%, fair P/E = 25x
> Fair Price = 25 x $5.00 = **$125.00**

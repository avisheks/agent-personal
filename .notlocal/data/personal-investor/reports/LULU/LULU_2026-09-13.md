## 12. Things the Market May Be Missing

### Positive Surprises (Upside Optionality)

**1. LULU Is Still Highly Profitable** **[INFERENCE]**
- 12.0% net margins place LULU in the top decile of all retailers globally. For comparison, Nike's net margin is ~10-12%, Gap's is ~3-5%, and most apparel retailers operate at 2-5%.
- The market is pricing LULU as if margins are about to collapse, but Q4 2025 margins were still 12%. The Q2 2026 EPS collapse may reflect seasonal/one-time factors rather than structural margin erosion.

**2. Brand Loyalty May Be Deeper Than Perceived** **[INFERENCE]**
- LULU's core customer (affluent, health-conscious, 25-45 female) has proven remarkably sticky through past brand challenges (the 2013 sheer-pants recall, leadership turnover, Mirror debacle).
- Community programs and ambassador networks create switching costs that pure-play competitors lack.
- Net Promoter Scores for LULU have historically been among the highest in retail.

**3. The Men's Business Is Underappreciated** **[INFERENCE]**
- LULU's men's category has been growing faster than women's and represents untapped potential.
- Men's athletic apparel is a $100B+ global market where LULU has minimal penetration.
- If men's reaches 30-40% of total revenue (from ~25% currently estimated), it could offset women's category saturation.

**4. Buyback Optionality at Distressed Prices** **[INFERENCE]**
- If management redirects CapEx savings into buybacks at ~$94/share, the accretion would be significant.
- $500M in buybacks at current prices retires ~5.3M shares (4.7% of float), materially boosting EPS even on flat net income.
- This is among the best possible times for LULU to be buying back stock -- but only if the business stabilizes.

**5. PE / Strategic Acquisition Target** **[INFERENCE]**
- A $10.6B market cap for a $10B+ revenue premium brand with 12% margins is attractive to PE firms.
- At 4.8x EV/EBITDA, LULU is within typical LBO valuation ranges (6-10x is standard; 4.8x is a bargain if cash flows are stable).
- Comparable acquisitions in the consumer/apparel space have occurred at significantly higher multiples.

### Negative Surprises (Downside Risks Underpriced)

**1. The Q2 2026 EPS Collapse May Be Structural** **[INFERENCE]**
- Q2 2026 EPS of $1.69 vs $2.60 in Q2 2025 is a -35% decline -- far worse than the -4.3% revenue decline in Q3 2026.
- This suggests operating leverage is working against LULU: small revenue declines translate to large earnings drops.
- If this negative operating leverage persists, the "cheap" 7.9x P/E will quickly become 12-15x as earnings fall.

**2. The Athleisure Category May Be Structurally Oversupplied** **[INFERENCE]**
- Every major brand (Nike, Adidas, Gap/Athleta, Under Armour, Alo, Vuori, On, Hoka) has expanded aggressively into athleisure.
- LULU's premium pricing ($98-$128 leggings) is under pressure as quality alternatives proliferate at lower price points.
- Amazon private labels offer functional equivalents at 50-70% lower prices.

**3. The 90.6% Drawdown Suggests Potential Structural Impairment** **[INFERENCE]**
- A 90.6% max drawdown is in the territory of stocks that undergo permanent impairment (Blackberry, GoPro, Peloton).
- The market's revealed preference -- through the price -- is that LULU may be in terminal decline.
- The burden of proof is on bulls to demonstrate this is cyclical rather than structural.

**4. Tariff and Supply Chain Exposure** **[INFERENCE]**
- LULU sources primarily from Vietnam and China. Escalating trade tensions or tariff increases would directly hit margins.
- Unlike diversified retailers, LULU's premium positioning means it cannot easily pass through tariff costs without risking demand destruction.
- This risk is partially priced in but could worsen under policy changes.

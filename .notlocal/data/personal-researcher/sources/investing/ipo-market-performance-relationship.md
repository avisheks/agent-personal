# U.S. Stock Market Performance Before and After Major IPOs

## Key Finding

Markets are typically STRONG before major IPOs, not weak. Companies deliberately time IPOs for favorable market conditions ("IPO windows"). The claim that "markets tank before big IPOs" is not supported by data.

## IPO Volume vs Market Conditions

- 2008 (bear market): 62 IPOs
- 2020 (post-COVID recovery): 480 IPOs
- 2021 (bull market peak): 1,035 IPOs (all-time record)
- 2022 (bear market): 181 IPOs (83% decline)
- 2023 (recovering): 154 IPOs

Academic confirmation: Loughran & Ritter found positive correlation between IPO volume and market level in 14 of 15 countries.

## IPO Window Concept

The "IPO window" is when market conditions favor public offerings. Companies POSTPONE IPOs during weak markets. Underwriters deliberately time for bull markets.

## Liquidity Drain Theory

- Mega-IPOs absorb $10-25B each
- JPMorgan: If SpaceX achieves $2T valuation with 50% float, passive funds would sell ~$950B to rebalance
- Bank of America: Private wealth clients at record-low 9.9% cash
- Effect more pronounced at SECTOR level than broad market level
- Examples: After Facebook IPO, Zynga dropped 13.4% in one day; After Alibaba, Amazon fell 2.1%

## IPO Boom as Market Top Signal

- 1999-2000: 480 IPOs preceded Nasdaq crash of ~78%
- 2021-2022: Record 1,035 IPOs preceded S&P -20%, Nasdaq -33%
- Statistical analysis: crash probability after IPO wave 20% vs normal 14.3%
- Counter-examples: 1983, 1986, 1993-1997 high IPO activity without crashes
- IPOs are SYMPTOM of euphoria, not CAUSE of crashes

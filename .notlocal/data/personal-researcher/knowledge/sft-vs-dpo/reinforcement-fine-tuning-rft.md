# Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)

**Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)** is a fine-tuning technique that uses reinforcement learning with a reward signal to optimize language models for complex objectives. Unlike traditional supervised approaches, RFT employs explicit rewards or penalties to guide model behavior, making it particularly effective for goal-directed, multi-step decision-making tasks. ^[fine-tuning-techniques-choosing-between-sft-dpo-and-rft-with-a-guide-to-dpo.md]

## Overview

RFT operates by having the model generate outputs for given prompts during training, with each output being evaluated for quality using a reward signal provided by a grader or reward model. The model's parameters are then updated to maximize the reward, creating an iterative feedback loop that encourages the model to improve reasoning or decision-making strategies. This approach differs from [[Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)]] and [[Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)]] by using explicit reward optimization rather than input-output pairs or preference comparisons. ^[fine-tuning-techniques-choosing-between-sft-dpo-and-rft-with-a-guide-to-dpo.md]

The training process involves optimizing the model to maximize expected reward, using predicted rewards generated by a reward model that is often guided by human judgment. This makes RFT particularly valuable when models need to make goal-directed, multi-step decisions, such as tool use or policy compliance. ^[how-to-fine-tune-ai-sft-dpo-and-rft-methods-cleverx-cleverx-blog.md]

## When to Use RFT

RFT is most effective in specific scenarios where other fine-tuning methods fall short:

### Ideal Use Cases
- Complex domain-specific tasks that require advanced reasoning
- Refining existing partial capabilities to foster emergent behaviors
- Tasks with measurable feedback signals
- Scenarios with limited explicit labels where reward signals can be defined
- Long-horizon tasks spanning multiple steps
- Tool-use sequences and workflow automation
- Policy compliance and safety-critical applications ^[fine-tuning-techniques-choosing-between-sft-dpo-and-rft-with-a-guide-to-dpo.md] ^[how-to-fine-tune-ai-sft-dpo-and-rft-methods-cleverx-cleverx-blog.md]

### Limitations
RFT is not suitable for:
- Tasks where the model has no initial skill or capability
- Tasks without clear feedback or measurable signals
- Simple, well-defined tasks that can be handled by supervised methods ^[fine-tuning-techniques-choosing-between-sft-dpo-and-rft-with-a-guide-to-dpo.md]

## Technical Implementation

### Reward Design
Successful RFT implementations often use decomposed rewards that balance multiple objectives:
- Accuracy components
- Safety measures  
- Efficiency metrics ^[how-to-fine-tune-ai-sft-dpo-and-rft-methods-cleverx-cleverx-blog.md]

### Training Patterns
Effective RFT deployment follows specific patterns:
- Start with offline training using conservative KL divergence controls
- Test controlled online updates in safe environments
- Use shadow deployments and canary releases to minimize risk ^[how-to-fine-tune-ai-sft-dpo-and-rft-methods-cleverx-cleverx-blog.md]

## Comparison with Other Methods

RFT differs significantly from other fine-tuning approaches in complexity and application:

| Method | Data Requirements | Complexity | Best For |
|--------|------------------|------------|----------|
| [[Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)]] | Labeled input-output pairs | Low | Simple, well-defined tasks |
| [[Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)]] | Human preference pairs | Medium | Aligning with human preferences |
| **RFT** | Defined rewards | High | Multi-step goals and agent workflows |

## Strengths and Challenges

### Strengths
- Handles long-horizon tasks effectively
- Flexible reward design allows for complex objective optimization
- Supports goal-directed behavior in multi-step scenarios ^[how-to-fine-tune-ai-sft-dpo-and-rft-methods-cleverx-cleverx-blog.md]

### Challenges
- Defining stable, meaningful rewards is difficult
- Risk of reward hacking where models learn shortcuts
- Requires careful monitoring and validation
- Higher computational requirements compared to other methods ^[how-to-fine-tune-ai-sft-dpo-and-rft-methods-cleverx-cleverx-blog.md]

## Risk Management

RFT implementations require careful attention to potential failure modes:

### Common Risks
- **Reward hacking**: Models learning to exploit reward function shortcuts rather than achieving intended objectives
- **Instability**: Performance variations across different training seeds
- **Overfitting**: Models becoming too specialized for evaluation metrics ^[how-to-fine-tune-ai-sft-dpo-and-rft-methods-cleverx-cleverx-blog.md]

### Mitigation Strategies
- Regular refresh of training data and reference models
- Adversarial probes and hidden holdout sets
- Variance checks across multiple training runs ^[how-to-fine-tune-ai-sft-dpo-and-rft-methods-cleverx-cleverx-blog.md]

## Relationship to RLHF

RFT is closely related to [[Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)]], sharing the reinforcement learning foundation but potentially differing in implementation details and reward signal sources. Both approaches use reward-based optimization to align model behavior with desired outcomes. ^[fine-tuning-techniques-choosing-between-sft-dpo-and-rft-with-a-guide-to-dpo.md]

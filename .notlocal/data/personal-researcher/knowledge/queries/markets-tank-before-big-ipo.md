## Short Answer

**No. Markets have historically been STRONG before big IPOs, not weak.**

- S&P 500 averaged **+4.6%** in the 90 days before the 6 largest IPOs (2012-2023)
- Every mega-IPO in the sample occurred during a bull market or at/near all-time highs
- IPO volume surges in strong markets (1,035 in 2021) and collapses in weak ones (62 in 2008, 181 in 2022)
- Companies and underwriters deliberately TIME their IPOs for strong markets ("IPO window")

The claim that "markets tank before big IPOs" gets the causality backwards.

## What About Liquidity Drain?

Mega-IPOs absorb $10-25B each, but relative to total U.S. market cap ($40-50T), this is ~0.05%. Effects are sector-level, not market-wide:
- After Facebook: Zynga -13.4%
- After Alibaba: Amazon -2.1%, eBay -1.4%
- Broad market impact: minimal

## The Real Signal: IPO Booms Predict Tops (Not Pre-IPO Declines)

The legitimate concern is different: IPO **clusters** may signal market **tops** — meaning declines come AFTER the boom, not before:
- 1999-2000: IPO frenzy → Nasdaq -78%
- 2021-2022: Record 1,035 IPOs → S&P -20%, Nasdaq -33%
- But crash probability after IPO wave is only ~20% vs 14% base rate
- Counter-examples: 1983-1997 had high IPO activity without crashes

## Why the Misconception Exists

People confuse two different claims:
1. "Markets decline BEFORE big IPOs" → **FALSE** (markets are strong before)
2. "Markets decline AFTER IPO booms" → **SOMETIMES TRUE** (20% vs 14% rate)

The second claim gets misremembered as the first.

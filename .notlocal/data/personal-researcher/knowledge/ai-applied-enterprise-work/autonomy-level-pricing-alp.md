# Autonomy-Level Pricing (ALP)

**Autonomy-Level Pricing (ALP)** is an emerging pricing model for AI services that tags resource units with autonomy levels to reflect how work is delivered rather than just what is delivered. ALP bridges the gap between traditional software-style licensing and transaction-based contracts by providing transparency into the degree of AI autonomy used to complete specific tasks. ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

## Overview

ALP addresses a fundamental challenge in AI pricing: traditional resource units (RUs) like "per ticket" or "per invoice" remain agnostic to whether work is performed by human analysts or AI agents. By augmenting familiar billing units with execution context, ALP enables more nuanced and fair pricing that aligns costs with the actual delivery method. ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

The model preserves procurement logic while adding intelligence-tagged units that give sourcing teams transparency without disrupting established frameworks. A claim processed at Autonomy Level 1 (AL1) is priced differently than one processed at Autonomy Level 3 (AL3), reflecting the different levels of human involvement and risk. ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

## Autonomy Level Framework

ALP operates on a five-tier autonomy scale:

- **AL0**: Fully manual execution with no AI involvement
- **AL1**: AI suggests actions, humans execute them
- **AL2**: AI executes actions, humans validate the results
- **AL3**: AI executes actions, humans audit exceptions only
- **AL4**: Fully autonomous execution, governed by policies and audits ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

Each level defines specific governance roles and execution behaviors. At AL1 and AL2, human involvement remains central with collaborative effort and validation requirements. At AL3, audit-on-exception makes autonomy viable while maintaining oversight. At AL4, full autonomy enables lower per-unit pricing but requires auditability and fallback obligations. ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

## Implementation Example

In finance record-to-report processes, ALP can be applied as follows:

- **Journal Entry Creation** (AL1): AI drafts entries, humans post them with human-in-the-loop governance
- **Account Reconciliation** (AL2): AI matches transactions, humans confirm with verification required
- **Variance Analysis** (AL3): AI surfaces anomalies with audit-on-exception governance
- **High-Volume General Ledger Posting** (AL4): AI posts within thresholds with retrospective audit ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

## Seven Pricing Principles

ALP adheres to seven core pricing principles that ensure viable commercial models:

1. **Fairness**: Value exchange remains equitable for both parties
2. **Variability**: Charges flex with consumption or measurable outcomes
3. **Consistency**: Pricing models stay stable and comparable across providers
4. **Competitiveness**: Prices can be benchmarked against market standards
5. **Incentives**: Contracts reward efficiency and innovation
6. **Risk Awareness**: Risk allocation is transparent as autonomy increases
7. **Proportionality**: Charges map to true cost drivers rather than proxies ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

## Benefits and Advantages

ALP provides several key advantages over traditional pricing models:

- **Transparent Alignment**: Price reflects execution maturity rather than arbitrary metrics
- **Provider Credit**: Suppliers receive recognition for delivering higher autonomy safely
- **Benchmarkable Comparisons**: Enables meaningful comparisons across suppliers
- **Audit Trails**: Satisfies governance and regulatory requirements
- **Preserved Familiarity**: Maintains resource unit anchors that procurement teams understand ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

The model enables buyers to see cost reductions as autonomy rises while allowing providers to retain margins without overcommitting upfront. Prices flex with real execution behavior rather than assumptions, and providers are rewarded for safely advancing autonomy while buyers capture savings without waiting for full maturity. ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

## Market Context

ALP emerges as a response to two dominant but problematic pricing patterns in 2025. Software-style deals require enterprises to pay for licenses and tokens while absorbing hidden costs for training, monitoring, and infrastructure. Transaction-based contracts price work per unit but obscure how that work is actually performed, creating tension between familiar pricing and unfamiliar execution models. ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

## Evolution and Recalibration

ALP is not intended as permanent scaffolding. Like early business process outsourcing contracts that required re-basing when automation matured, ALP requires regular recalibration as the [[agentic-ai]] frontier continues to advance. Multipliers tied to current autonomy levels should not harden into outdated labor proxies as technology evolves. ^[2025-isg-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption-report.pdf]

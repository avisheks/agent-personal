# Market Performance Before Major IPOs

Market performance before major Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) demonstrates a counterintuitive pattern that challenges common assumptions about market timing and liquidity effects. Contrary to popular belief that markets weaken before large IPOs, historical data reveals that markets are typically strong during periods preceding major public offerings.

## The IPO Window Phenomenon

The concept of the "[[IPO Window Concept]]" explains why markets tend to be strong before major IPOs rather than weak. Companies and their underwriters deliberately time IPOs to coincide with favorable market conditions, creating what academics call IPO windows when market sentiment supports public offerings. During weak market periods, companies typically postpone their IPOs rather than proceed with unfavorable pricing. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

This timing strategy is supported by academic research from Loughran & Ritter, who found positive correlation between IPO volume and market levels in 14 of 15 countries studied. The data demonstrates that IPO activity serves as a symptom of market strength rather than a cause of market weakness. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

## Historical Performance Data

Analysis of the largest IPOs in U.S. history reveals consistent patterns of market strength in the periods leading up to major offerings. The S&P 500 performance before the six largest IPOs shows average gains of 0.5% in the 30 days prior, 2.0% in the 60 days prior, and 4.6% in the 90 days prior to the IPO dates. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

Notable examples include Alibaba's September 2014 IPO, which raised $25 billion during a period when the S&P 500 gained 2.4% over the preceding 90 days, and Rivian's November 2021 IPO, which occurred after the market had gained 4.8% over the prior three months. Even during more challenging periods, such as ARM's September 2023 IPO, the market showed resilience with only modest declines. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

## IPO Volume and Market Cycles

The relationship between IPO volume and market conditions provides additional evidence for the strength-before-IPOs pattern. During the 2008 bear market, only 62 IPOs occurred, while the bull market peak of 2021 saw a record 1,035 IPOs. The dramatic 83% decline in IPO activity during 2022's bear market, dropping to just 181 IPOs, further illustrates how companies avoid public offerings during unfavorable market conditions. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

The [[IPO Volume as Market Sentiment Indicator]] demonstrates this relationship across multiple market cycles, with academic confirmation from Loughran & Ritter showing positive correlation between IPO volume and market levels in 14 of 15 countries studied. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

## Liquidity Considerations and Sector Effects

While broad market performance tends to be positive before major IPOs, the [[Liquidity Drain Theory]] introduces important nuances regarding liquidity effects. Mega-IPOs can absorb $10-25 billion each, creating potential liquidity drains that may affect specific sectors more than the overall market. JPMorgan analysis suggests that if a company like SpaceX achieved a $2 trillion valuation with 50% float, passive funds would need to sell approximately $950 billion in other holdings to rebalance portfolios. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

These liquidity effects manifest more prominently at the sector level than in broad market indices. Historical examples include Zynga's 13.4% single-day drop following Facebook's IPO and Amazon's 2.1% decline after Alibaba's public offering, demonstrating how large IPOs can create sector-specific pressure even during periods of overall market strength. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

## Market Top Indicators

The relationship between IPO activity and market cycles extends beyond immediate pre-IPO performance to longer-term market timing considerations. Historical analysis reveals that periods of intense IPO activity, such as the 480 IPOs in 1999-2000 and the record 1,035 IPOs in 2021, have preceded significant market corrections. However, statistical analysis shows crash probability after IPO waves at 20% compared to normal conditions at 14.3%, indicating correlation rather than causation. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

The [[IPO Boom as Market Top Signal]] concept recognizes that while IPO booms can indicate market euphoria, counter-examples from 1983, 1986, and 1993-1997 demonstrate that high IPO activity does not automatically predict market crashes, reinforcing the interpretation that IPOs serve as symptoms of market euphoria rather than direct causes of subsequent weakness. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

## Implications for Market Timing

The empirical evidence suggests that investors should reconsider assumptions about market weakness preceding major IPOs. The [[IPO Market Timing and Performance]] relationship indicates that companies strategically time their public offerings during favorable market conditions, making IPO announcements more likely to coincide with market strength rather than weakness. This pattern holds across different market cycles and geographic regions, providing a robust framework for understanding the relationship between IPO activity and market performance. ^[market-performance-before-major-ipos.md]

# IPO Boom as Market Top Signal

IPO booms correlate with market tops, but the signal is unreliable. The crash probability after an IPO wave is ~20% vs a base rate of ~14.3% — meaningful but not deterministic. ^[ipo-market-performance-relationship.md]

## Canonical Examples (Signal Worked)

- **1999-2000**: 480 IPOs ($69.1B raised) preceded Nasdaq crash of ~78%
- **2021-2022**: Record 1,035 IPOs, S&P peaked Jan 2022, then fell ~20%; Nasdaq fell ~33%
- **Alibaba (Sep 2014)**: Coincided with S&P all-time high; market pulled back 7% within a month

## Counter-Examples (Signal Failed)

- **1983, 1986, 1993-1997**: High IPO activity without subsequent crashes
- **ICBC (2006)**: Shanghai Composite rose 237% in 12 months after
- **Meta (2012)**: S&P 500 rose 25% in 12 months after

## Interpretation

IPOs are a **symptom** of financial euphoria and late-cycle risk appetite, not a **cause** of market crashes. The mechanism:
1. Bull market → investor confidence rises → companies rush to IPO at peak valuations
2. IPO boom reflects maximum optimism → mean reversion eventually occurs
3. But timing is unpredictable (bull runs can extend years past IPO peaks)

# Midterm Election Market Weakness

U.S. stock markets have historically delivered below-average returns during midterm election years (Year 2 of the presidential term). The S&P 500 has averaged -0.1% in midterm years compared to its long-term annual average of approximately 8.9%. ^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Historical Performance Data

### S&P 500 Midterm Year Returns (1960-2024)
- **Average return**: -0.1%
- **Median return**: -0.3%
- **Negative years**: 9 out of 16 (56%)
- **Vs long-term average**: ~8.9% annual return ^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

### Nasdaq Performance
- **Average midterm year return**: -0.5%
- **Higher volatility**: More extreme swings due to higher beta
- **Notable crashes**: 2022 (-33.1%), 2002 (-31.5%) ^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Worst Midterm Years

The most severe market declines during midterm election years often coincided with broader economic or geopolitical crises:

| Year | S&P 500 Return | Context |
|------|---------------|---------|
| 1974 | -30.2% | Oil embargo, Watergate scandal |
| 2002 | -21.1% | Dot-com bust aftermath, 9/11 impact |
| 2022 | -14.5% | Aggressive Federal Reserve rate hikes |
| 1966 | -12.8% | Inflation concerns, Vietnam War escalation |
| 1962 | -9.3% | Cuban Missile Crisis |

^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Notable Exceptions

Several midterm years bucked the trend with strong positive returns:

| Year | S&P 500 Return | Context |
|------|---------------|---------|
| 1998 | +23.5% | Dot-com bubble expansion |
| 1986 | +19.4% | Continued bull market |
| 1982 | +18.8% | Volcker Federal Reserve rate cuts |
| 2014 | +12.7% | Quantitative easing-fueled bull market |
| 2010 | +10.5% | QE2 economic recovery |
| 2006 | +10.8% | Housing bubble peak |

^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Underlying Causes

### Policy Uncertainty
Markets face uncertainty about which party will control Congress after the midterm elections, creating unclear policy direction. ^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

### Front-Loaded Fiscal Pain
New presidents typically implement unpopular but necessary policies early in their terms, with the economic pain often peaking during Year 2. ^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

### Monetary Policy Cycles
Midterm weakness often coincides with Federal Reserve tightening cycles, as seen in 1966, 1974, and 2022. ^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

### Peak Volatility Timing
Market volatility typically peaks approximately 60 trading days before Election Day, contributing to overall market stress during midterm years. ^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Statistical Significance

U.S. Bank research analyzing 31 midterm elections over 125 years found that while the pattern exists, it is not statistically significant due to high variability and small sample sizes. In 11 of 31 midterm years, external economic factors overwhelmed any election-related effects. ^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Related Patterns

Midterm election market weakness is part of the broader [[Presidential Election Cycle Theory]], which identifies a four-year pattern where Year 2 (midterm) typically shows the weakest performance, followed by the strongest returns in Year 3. This weakness is often followed by the [[Post-Midterm Rally]], where markets have historically delivered above-average returns in the year following midterm elections. ^[investing/midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

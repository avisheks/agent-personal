# Post-Midterm Rally

The **post-midterm rally** is one of the most consistent patterns in U.S. market history. Markets have historically delivered strong returns in the 12 months following a midterm election, with the S&P 500 averaging +12.3% in post-midterm years from 1960-2024. ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Key Statistics

The post-midterm rally demonstrates remarkable consistency across multiple decades:

- **S&P 500 average post-midterm year return (1960-2024): +12.3%**
- **Hit rate: 88%** (14 of 16 post-midterm years positive)
- **First 3 months after midterm Election Day: +6.5% average**
- **Since 1950: positive 12-month returns followed EVERY midterm election**
- **Nasdaq Year 3 average since 1971: +28.8%** ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Historical Performance Data

The pattern becomes clear when examining specific midterm-to-post-midterm transitions. The strongest rallies typically follow the weakest midterm years, creating dramatic performance swings:

### Strongest Post-Midterm Rallies

| Midterm Year | Midterm Return | Post-Midterm Year | Return | Performance Swing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1974 | -30.2% | 1975 | +22.2% | +52.4pp |
| 2002 | -21.1% | 2003 | +20.6% | +41.7pp |
| 1994 | -3.8% | 1995 | +32.1% | +35.9pp |
| 2022 | -14.5% | 2023 | +18.3% | +32.8pp |
| 2018 | -8.0% | 2019 | +21.8% | +29.8pp |

The 1995 rally of +32.1% following 1994's modest -3.8% decline represents one of the strongest post-midterm performances on record. ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

### Nasdaq Outperformance

The technology-heavy Nasdaq typically exhibits more extreme versions of the post-midterm pattern due to its higher beta characteristics. Notable Nasdaq post-midterm years include 1999 (+85.6%), 2003 (+50.0%), and 2023 (+43.4%). ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Notable Exceptions

While the post-midterm rally has an exceptional track record, two significant counter-examples highlight the pattern's limitations:

- **1987** (post-1986 midterm): -8.9% — The Black Monday crash in October 1987 overwhelmed the typical post-midterm dynamics
- **2011** (post-2010 midterm): -3.1% — The European debt crisis and U.S. credit downgrade created external headwinds

Notably, both failed rallies followed midterm years that were actually positive (1986: +19.4%, 2010: +10.5%), breaking the typical "weak midterm → strong recovery" sequence. ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Underlying Mechanisms

Several factors contribute to the post-midterm rally phenomenon:

**Uncertainty Resolution**: Once congressional control is decided, the policy direction becomes clearer, reducing political uncertainty that markets dislike. ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

**[[Divided Government Market Effect]]**: Gridlock following midterm elections has historically favored markets, as it reduces the likelihood of disruptive policy changes. ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

**Economic Stimulus Cycle**: Incumbent administrations typically begin boosting the economy in preparation for the upcoming presidential re-election campaign. ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

**Mean Reversion**: After weak midterm performance, beaten-down valuations often create attractive buying opportunities for investors. ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Statistical Considerations

Despite the pattern's consistency, formal statistical testing by U.S. Bank research found it is not statistically significant given the high variability and small sample sizes involved. In 11 of 31 midterm years studied, external factors overwhelmed any election-related effects. ^[midterm-elections-market-performance.md]

## Related Patterns

The post-midterm rally is part of the broader [[Presidential Election Cycle Theory]], which identifies Year 3 (post-midterm) as the strongest-performing year in the four-year presidential cycle. This pattern connects to [[Midterm Election Market Weakness]], as the post-midterm strength often represents a recovery from midterm-year underperformance.

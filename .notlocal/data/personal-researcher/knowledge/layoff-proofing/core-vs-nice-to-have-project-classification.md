# Core vs Nice-to-Have Project Classification

**Core vs Nice-to-Have Project Classification** is a strategic framework for evaluating the business criticality of technical projects, particularly important for individual contributors seeking to recession-proof their careers in the technology sector.

## Overview

The classification system distinguishes between projects that are essential to business operations ("core") versus those that provide additional value but are not critical to survival ("nice-to-have"). This distinction becomes particularly crucial during economic downturns when organizations prioritize resources toward revenue-generating or strategically essential initiatives. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

## The Core Strategy

The "Core" Strategy represents a defensive career approach where technical professionals ensure their contributions align with projects that directly impact the company's profit and loss statement. Rather than working exclusively on speculative or experimental projects, individuals should maintain involvement in core initiatives that the business "can't-live-without." ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

This strategy requires auditing current project assignments to determine whether they fall into the "nice-to-have" or "can't-live-without" category. If working on speculative projects, professionals should ensure they also contribute to core projects that directly feed the P&L. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

## Business Impact Measurement

The framework emphasizes transitioning from technical metrics to business outcomes when evaluating project value. Instead of reporting purely technical achievements, professionals should map their contributions to bottom-line results using what's called "The ROI Lens." ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

For example, rather than stating "Deployed a new architecture," the business-focused framing would be "Reduced inference latency by 30%, resulting in $X annual compute savings." This approach demonstrates direct financial impact rather than just technical capability. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

## Strategic Implementation

### Value Anchor Positioning

The classification system supports the broader goal of transitioning from a "delivery engine" to a "value anchor" within an organization. This involves moving beyond pure technical execution to becoming indispensable through business-critical contributions. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

### Risk Mitigation

During periods of organizational restructuring or economic uncertainty, projects classified as "nice-to-have" face higher risk of cancellation or deprioritization. By focusing on core projects, technical professionals can better position themselves as essential to business operations rather than optional resources. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

## Relationship to Career Resilience

The Core vs Nice-to-Have classification integrates with broader career resilience strategies, including the development of [[force-multiplier-engineering]] capabilities and [[trade-off-literacy]] for technical decision-making. It also connects to [[revenue-tied-technical-work]] and other business-critical technical initiatives that clearly demonstrate organizational value. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

The framework supports the [[value-anchor-strategy]] by helping technical professionals identify which projects will make them structurally important to their organizations rather than easily replaceable resources. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

## Practical Application

### Project Audit Process

Technical professionals should regularly evaluate their current assignments using this classification framework. The audit involves assessing whether each project directly contributes to company revenue, operational stability, or strategic survival requirements. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

### Portfolio Balance

While maintaining focus on core projects, professionals can still engage with innovative or experimental work. The key is ensuring that speculative projects complement rather than replace involvement in business-critical initiatives that directly impact the organization's financial performance. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

## Integration with Business Impact Mapping

The classification system works in conjunction with [[business-impact-mapping]] techniques to help technical professionals articulate the value of their contributions in terms that resonate with business stakeholders and decision-makers during budget allocation and resource planning cycles. ^[recession-proofing-guide.md]

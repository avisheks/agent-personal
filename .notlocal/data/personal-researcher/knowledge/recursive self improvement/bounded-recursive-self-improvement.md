# Bounded Recursive Self-Improvement

**Bounded Recursive Self-Improvement** refers to AI systems that can modify and improve aspects of themselves or their training processes within constrained, well-defined parameters, without achieving full autonomous intelligence explosion. This represents a practical, engineering-oriented approach to recursive self-improvement that focuses on specific workflows rather than general intelligence enhancement.

## Overview

Bounded recursive self-improvement operates through a feedback loop where an AI system proposes modifications to its own training code, runs experiments, evaluates results, and iteratively keeps improvements. Unlike theoretical "hard RSI" scenarios that envision fully autonomous intelligence explosions, bounded RSI focuses on narrow, measurable improvements within specific domains such as hyperparameter optimization, code generation, and experiment automation. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

The concept has gained prominence through practical implementations that demonstrate AI systems autonomously improving training pipelines, optimizing models with minimal human intervention, and operating within constrained experimental loops. These systems represent a transition from humans manually tuning models to AI agents autonomously running experiments, editing code, evaluating results, and iterating. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Technical Implementation

Modern bounded RSI systems typically focus on optimizing specific aspects of AI development workflows rather than rewriting entire architectures independently. Common applications include training pipeline optimization, hyperparameter search, code generation, experiment automation, and agentic software engineering. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

A concrete example of this approach is AutoResearch, an open-source project where an [[LLM-as-Judge Quality Scoring]] system proposes changes to ML training code, runs experiments, evaluates metrics, keeps improvements, and repeats automatically. Such systems have demonstrated the ability to run hundreds of experiments autonomously, modify training code, and optimize models with minimal human intervention. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Distinction from Full RSI

Bounded recursive self-improvement explicitly differs from theoretical "full recursive self-improvement" in several key ways. Current systems operate within narrow, constrained parameters and focus on specific optimization tasks rather than general intelligence enhancement. They do not rewrite their entire architecture independently, invent radically new intelligence, or escape human control. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

Research papers on the topic, such as the Self-Taught Optimizer (STOP) paper, have shown language-model-driven systems improving code scaffolds recursively while explicitly noting that this represents only partial forms of RSI rather than full recursive self-improvement. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Industry Applications

Bounded RSI has particular relevance for domains where workflows are measurable and automatable. In adtech and foundation models, RSI-like systems can optimize model evaluation, reward optimization, feature engineering, retrieval tuning, prompt optimization, synthetic data generation, policy tuning, and agent-based experimentation. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

The approach addresses key bottlenecks in AI progress, including researcher productivity, experimentation speed, infrastructure tuning, data curation, and evaluation loops. By enabling AI agents to accelerate these workflows, bounded RSI can increase frontier model development speed, reduce human requirements per experiment, and compound research throughput. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Gradual Development Path

Rather than emerging as a sudden singularity, bounded recursive self-improvement is expected to develop gradually through an incremental sequence. This progression might include stages from basic coding assistance to AI agents fixing bugs, running experiments, optimizing training, designing architectures, and eventually enabling partially autonomous AI research and development. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

This gradual approach represents the distinction between "hard RSI" (fully autonomous intelligence explosion) and "soft RSI" (AI accelerating AI engineering workflows), with the industry already moving toward the latter approach. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Historical Context

The concept builds on decades of theoretical work, including I.J. Good's "intelligence explosion" concept from 1965, Seed AI ideas from Eliezer Yudkowsky, and Nick Bostrom's Superintelligence. However, modern bounded RSI represents a more practical engineering approach compared to these earlier theoretical frameworks. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

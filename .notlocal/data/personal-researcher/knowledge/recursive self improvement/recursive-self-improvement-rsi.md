# Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)

**Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)** refers to AI systems that can modify or improve some part of themselves, creating a feedback loop where the improved system becomes better at making future improvements. This concept has evolved from theoretical speculation to practical engineering systems that can autonomously optimize training pipelines, generate code, and run experiments.

## Core Concept

Recursive self-improvement operates through a fundamental feedback loop where an AI model improves training code or research processes, becomes more capable through these improvements, and then applies its enhanced capabilities to make even better improvements in subsequent iterations. This creates a compounding effect where each cycle of improvement potentially accelerates the next. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

The concept encompasses a spectrum from narrow optimization tasks to theoretical "intelligence explosions." Current implementations focus on bounded, measurable improvements rather than fundamental architectural changes. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Historical Background

The theoretical foundations of RSI trace back to I.J. Good's "intelligence explosion" concept from 1965, followed by Seed AI ideas from Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nick Bostrom's work in Superintelligence. These early formulations focused on the potential for rapid, unbounded intelligence growth. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

Modern technical implementations have emerged through systems like Bounded Recursive Self-Improvement and the Self-Taught Optimizer (STOP), which demonstrate practical applications of RSI principles within constrained experimental environments. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Practical Implementation

Contemporary RSI systems typically operate through automated research loops where language models propose changes to machine learning training code, execute experiments, evaluate metrics, retain improvements, and repeat the process with minimal human intervention. These systems can run hundreds of experiments autonomously while modifying training code and optimizing models. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

A notable example is AutoResearch, an open-source project that demonstrates practical RSI by allowing systems to continuously improve code and research workflows through automated experimentation cycles. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Current Scope and Limitations

Modern RSI implementations focus on specific, measurable tasks rather than comprehensive self-modification. Current systems excel at optimizing training pipelines, conducting hyperparameter searches, generating code, automating experiments, and performing agentic software engineering tasks. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

However, these systems do not rewrite their entire architectures independently, invent radically new forms of intelligence, or operate beyond human oversight. Even advanced papers on the topic explicitly acknowledge that current systems represent only partial forms of RSI. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Industry Relevance

RSI has become particularly relevant as AI progress faces bottlenecks in researcher productivity, experimentation speed, infrastructure tuning, data curation, and evaluation loops. AI agents that can accelerate these workflows enable faster frontier model development, reduce human requirements per experiment, and create compounding research throughput. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

For applications in advertising technology and foundation models, RSI-like systems can automate model evaluation, reward optimization, feature engineering, retrieval tuning, prompt optimization, synthetic data generation, policy tuning, and agent-based experimentation. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Soft vs Hard RSI

The field distinguishes between "soft RSI" and "hard RSI." Soft RSI involves AI systems accelerating AI engineering workflows through bounded improvements to specific processes. Hard RSI refers to fully autonomous intelligence explosions with unbounded self-modification capabilities. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

Current industry developments focus primarily on soft RSI, with practical systems that enhance existing workflows rather than fundamentally restructuring intelligence itself. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

## Future Trajectory

The evolution toward RSI may follow a gradual progression from coding assistance tools to AI agents that fix bugs, run experiments, optimize training processes, design architectures, and eventually enable partially autonomous AI research and development. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

This progression suggests that RSI will emerge incrementally through improved automation of existing research and development processes rather than as a sudden technological breakthrough. ^[rsi-andrej.md]

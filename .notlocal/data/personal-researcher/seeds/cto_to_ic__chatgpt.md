# CTOs and Tech Leaders Moving Back to IC Roles

What is happening is not a mass exodus. It is a selective rerouting: some senior technical leaders are moving back into hands-on roles when the work is more interesting, orgs are flatter, or the executive job becomes too detached from engineering. Stack Overflow’s discussion of Mitchell Hashimoto notes that moving from CEO back to IC is uncommon, which fits the broader pattern: it is real, but not universal.[1]

The main forces behind it are pretty clear. Companies are flattening layers and asking leaders to stay closer to execution; Reuters reported Intel’s CEO was streamlining leadership and shifting major chip groups to report directly to him.[2] AI is also increasing the value of deep technical contributors, and the prestige of high-end IC roles is rising in frontier labs. In that environment, some leaders decide they would rather build than manage.

There are also visible examples. HashiCorp’s Mitchell Hashimoto moved into a full-time IC role and said he would work on product teams or new ideas while still participating in big-picture decisions.[3] Reddit discussions from experienced engineers echo the same reasons people cite for moving from management back to IC work: less overhead, more direct impact, and a stronger fit with deep technical work.[4]

My read: this is less “executives are failing” and more “the industry is revaluing hands-on technical impact.” The strongest version of this move is intentional and lateral. The weaker version is when an organization flattens and the executive role loses its reason to exist.
